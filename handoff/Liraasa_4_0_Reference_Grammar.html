Part1

Part 1: What Liraasa is

liraasa is the ascended form of lira, song: “song of the first stars”. A goddess-centric language, built for singing.

2,468 headwords: 506 roots and 1,962 compounds.

The grammar in eight lines

  1. Every vowel is its own syllable; stress falls on the second-to-last. Spelling is sound.
  2. Every headword has seven slots by rule: a noun, its ascended and negative nouns, an adjective for each, and a verb.
  3. A compound is two headwords written as one, head last: luno + lumi = lunolumi, moonlight.
  4. Word order is subject, verb, object; tense and every operator stand before the verb.
  5. Adjectives come before the noun, and a noun never modifies a noun: make the first an adjective.
  6. Two copulas: ke for what is so now, ka for what is so by nature.
  7. A question keeps statement order and ends in ki.
  8. -ri makes a plural, -ti a little or dear thing. In X de Y, Y owns X, unless X is a pronoun or a name.

First words

  • yeko hello
  • voge goodbye / parting
  • zabo yes / affirmative
  • kepi no
  • fisa please
  • yade sorry / pardon
  • lorejuba thank you (short, native)
  • Lorejuba ala taivo. Thank you [heart-gift to you].

Part 2: Sound and singing

The spelling is phonetic: what you see is what you sing.

AspectRule
Vowelsa e i o u (pure); adjacent vowels are separate syllables
Lettersl m n r s v y; b d p t g h z k c f j (sh, ch only in colour/number words)
Stresspenultimate syllable

Vowels are pure, as in Spanish or Italian. l m n s v b d p t f k sound as in English; y as in "yes"; r is a single tap, as in Spanish "pero"; g is always hard, as in "go"; h as in "hope"; z as in "zone". c is always "ch" as in "chant", before every vowel: ca = cha, co = cho, and likewise ce, ci, cu. j is the voiced partner of c, as in "jewel". sh is as in "shade"; ch, written only in a few colour and number words, is the same sound as c. 2.0:G43

Adjacent vowels never merge. A consonant between vowels starts the next syllable; of two, the first closes the syllable before. Capitals mark the stress:

Writing for song

  • Stress and the beat. Every word falls from its stressed syllable to a light last one: set the stressed syllable on a strong beat and let the last fall on a weak one. The small words are short and unstressed; let them fill the weak beats.
  • Neighbouring vowels. Every written vowel is its own syllable (0.8:R8), but in singing neighbouring vowels may glide into one note: benaasa may be sung be-na-a-sa or be-naa-sa. The spelling never changes; the songwriter chooses the count the melody needs. 2.0:G44 0.8:R8
  • Rhyme. A rhyme runs from the stressed vowel to the end of the word: devi, nevi and jurevi rhyme on -evi. A compound ends in its head, so lira and daigolira share the whole rhyme: an identity rhyme, the same word sounding twice, and a family counts it once. Counted by the root each word ends in, across headwords, pronouns and small words, there are 35 rhyme families. Five of them:
  • -evi: jurevi, devi, nevi
  • -era: vera, mera
  • -ivo: rivo, maivo, saivo, taivo
  • -igo: daigo, ligo
  • -avu: nuravu, navu

Every adjective ends in -san, so adjectives at the ends of lines rhyme in five sets, by the vowel before it: -asan 197 (lirasan, benasan) · -esan 117 (loresan, sovesan) · -isan 167 (vanisan, lumisan) · -osan 168 (lunosan, melosan) · -usan 163 (velusan, ramusan).

Licences of verse

  • baba may be omitted only in verse. 0.8:R5
  • In verse, once the time has been set, the bare present may narrate past events. 1.2:G31
  • After a number or quantifier the plural -ri is optional: choose by sound and meter 1.2:G28.

Sound follows meaning

For new and respelled roots: prefer CVCV; l m n r v for sacred/tender/lyrical; b d g k p t z h for bodily/everyday/harsh; playful b c p for beloved small creatures; related meanings may share a consonant frame; no suffix-look-alike endings (-ti -ri -na -vu -yo -asa); at least two letters from every root and small word except deliberate families. Not retroactive. 2.1:G57

Names

A foreign name is not translated. It is copied as closely as Liraasa letters allow, choosing among near spellings by how the name sounds and sings. 2.0:G45 A name may take the affectionate -ti 0.8:R12.

Peter becomes Peta; Pietri becomes Petri.

Ascended is the ultimate, cosmic degree, for good or ill: lumiasa Her Divine Light; narvoasa infernal darkness, demons, narvoasasan demonic, evil. Negative is the absence or the reversal.

Many cells are empty. 11,909 of the 17,276 forms have no meaning: the lexicon marks them unused, and this guide strikes them through. devi, goddess, has no verb: devida is an empty form, and an empty form is not a word.

Compounds

  • MODIFIER + HEAD, written as one word, head last; the spelling is the two joined. A compound is one word, never two nouns side by side.

Registers

Side nouns as prepositions

nazru (behind), zavme (in front of), silda (beside), cemnu (in the middle of), lizma (left of) and rizno (right of) may stand bare before a noun phrase as prepositions, or after a verb as adverbs: Li lavra semnesia nazru li nimu. The bird sits behind the home. saivo de melonulu venreva nazru gase baba lira de ruhesonbu. Her melody comes behind, like a song of thunder. 2.2:G67

Numbers

Part 5: The clause

A fixed order, anchored on the verb. Only the subject and the verb are required.

The core order

FRAME · SUBJECT · TENSE · ne · MODAL · VERB · OBJECT · ADJUNCTS · ATTITUDE · ki
Eli-krono, maivo lirala.
Now I sing.
Maivo po ne somusia.
I did not sleep.
Taivo futu relnura ki?
Will you come back?
Maivo lirala poye.
Perhaps I sing.
  • Operators stand before the verb, in the order of the full table below. ne doli tu yesta pendi vinto stand immediately before the constituent they scope over (noun phrase, verb, adjective, number, clause-introducer such as si). Scope runs left over right 1.0:G1: ne negates what follows it.
  • One word per slot, at most 4 before the verb. These never share a clause 1.0:G16: hiro + roni · hiro + tefa · teki + roni · teki + tefa · telvo + tefa · vazdi + tefa · fesmo + tefa · zoke + seye · zoke + mufe · zoke + poye · zoke + pifu · tavki + fututi · hoyo + yasmo.
!Take care

In X de Y, Y owns X: li bumre de li cilni, the bread of the city. But a pronoun or a personal name on the left is the owner: taivo de lira, your song 1.2:G27. A kinship noun (pilo, mare …) is never a name for this: father’s bread is li bumre de pilo 2.5:G95. A pronoun owns wherever it stands: Pilo de maivo po lirala. My father sang 1.2:G32. With two names, the left one owns. Chains group from the left: X de Y de Z is (X de Y) de Z 2.5:G96.

Copulas

ke is / are: holds now, or for a time · ka is / are: lasting, defining, by nature · kapoki previously was, no longer is; contrasts with po ka (was, no claim about now) · loya become · miyo existential there is / are; after a subject, absolute being: exists (3.0:G132)

ke and ka mark one contrast for every kind of predicate, adjective, noun phrase or place: ke holds now or for a time, ka is lasting, defining, by nature: Li kavbikova ke su li tabu. The basket is on the table (right now). Maivo ka lirayo. I am a singer (it’s who I am). 2.5:G93 po ke is “was, at that time”, po ka “was”, with no claim about now, and kapoki “was, and no longer is”: Saivo kapoki benasan. They were good, and no longer are. 2.5:G94

A predicate adjective may stand bare, with no copula, only in the present and with no operators: Nela vanisan. The person is beautiful. Anything more needs ke or ka 2.5:G128. gome passivises any verb attested with an object; without it the subject is the doer, except for the listed change-of-state verbs 2.5:G110. Valency: CAUSER [TENSE] [noma] [ne] [MODAL] … vaku/lesvu/tekvu ACTOR [ne] [APPROACH] [PHASE] [EFFORT] VERB [OBJECT] 2.5:G109.

The noun phrase

[QUANTIFIER] [li|eli|ola|OWNER de] [NUMBER] [ADJECTIVES] NOUN[-ti][-ri] [de …] [raho CLAUSE]

Adjectives before the noun; a noun never modifies a noun 0.8:G7. toda N is every N; toda li N is all the N.

li may be omitted in everyday speech as well as verse when the referent is obvious; never when ambiguous. 0.8:R5

Partitive de. After a number, a quantifier or a superlative, de means of / among, and the ownership rules do not apply: Una de maivori futu relnura. One of us will return. Fractions are the same construction 2.1:G53.

Linking clauses

ata and · nara but / however · oro or · si if · sina unless · kasa because · kopisa so that / in order to · nevi although · fime complementizer: that · raho who / that / which (relative) · sidu infinitive / chaining · kube when (subordinate) · nayo while (subordinate time) · tuni until · sige since (time) · kureli as soon as

A clause with its own verb needs a linker. fime opens “that …” (the demonstrative is ola); raho opens a relative, and one that does not end the sentence is closed by a comma when it has its own object or adjunct: Li sabeyo raho vismava eli, ne yehu vimova li vordu. The sage who studies this did not witness the word. 2.5:G115 sidu joins two verbs sharing a subject and never takes a copula; purpose is always kopisa 2.5:G113; ala is toward, never the infinitive “to”.

Maivo sabesia fime taivo lirala.
I know that you sing.
Li nela raho lirala ka benasan.
The person who sings is good.
Maivo desida sidu lirala.
I want to sing.
Maivo desida fime taivo lisrola.
I want you to stay.
  • sidu keeps one subject; a second verb with its own subject goes in a fime clause 2.1:G50. An unmarked subordinate clause takes the main clause’s tense, whatever its linker; sige clauses always mark their own 2.5:G118.
  • Purpose. kopisa + a bare verb phrase with the same subject, + a clause with a different one: Maivo po venreva eli-pago kopisa lirala. I came here to sing. Maivo lirala kopisa taivo rilura. I sing so that you dance. 2.5:G113
  • A relative on a prepositional object: raho opens the clause and the preposition keeps its place, followed by a resumptive pronoun (ito/itori, saivo/saivori): Li nimu raho maivo po lirala en ito, ka vanisan. The home in which I sang is beautiful. 2.1:G52 So for a possessor (PRONOUN de NOUN) and a time or place (the preposition + ito): li krono raho maivo po lirala en ito the time when I sang 2.5:G120
  • Evidentials: with none, the sentence makes no claim about its source. Inside fime, the evidential reports the main-clause subject’s evidence; in a question it asks for the listener’s source 2.1:G51.
  • Hopeful purpose. kopisa + a clause whose verb carries nosi: Maivo lirala, kopisa taivo nosi rilura. I sing, so that perhaps you’ll dance. fime always introduces a content clause, “that” 2.5:G116.
  • Headless relatives. With no noun before it, raho is “the one who, that which”, as subject, object or complement: Maivo lirala raho taivo amora. I sing what you love. saivo po farufasbifa kube saivo ramura raho maivo po ke. She blushed when she remembered what I had been. A direct question may also open with raho, “who, what”, and then ends in ki, as the album asks: raho povi sabesia baba kasi polvasan sove ki? Who can know a more powerful peace? ki-nela and ki-coda stay the ordinary question words 2.3:G74.
  • Easy to …, ready to …. An adjective may take sidu + verb; the thing the adjective describes is the subject of the clause and the verb’s understood object, with no dummy ito: Trana ke ruli levasan sidu zeldoza. Change is very easy to resist. 2.5:G111
  • Before and after a clause. kroti and krotiapo may open a clause, “before …” and “after …”; the clause inherits tense as after kube 0.8:R6: kroti maivo lesvu taivo ke li sevusan ruhe. Before I let you be the ocean storm. Before a measure they keep 2.1:G55; before li + a noun, their older meaning 2.2:G66.
  • A line that continues. ata may open a line that carries on from the one before, “And …”, as oro may: ata maivo lemula saivo de sizosan lumi. And I learn his shimmering light. 2.3:G72 1.2:G25
  • Means, medium, company, manner. One test per word: yuta a tool, means or substance handled or used up; en a medium or state one is in (a manner seen as a medium): saivo po senosia en baba melosan valla. She spoke in a musical voice. ko a companion, or a feeling that goes with the act (ko amor, with love); la + adjective the quality of the action, after the verb and object 2.5:G104 2.5:G105.
  • Motion prefixes. va-, du-, taro-, yave- and revo- go on any verb, with compositional meanings; a listed meaning wins: Maivo revo-sabesia taivo. I recognize you. So pagopa takes them too: taivo ne taro-pagopa, nara lalfola hupi li lelde, you do not go across, but float above the edge, 2.5:G121

Questions, exclamations, wishes

A yes/no question keeps statement order and ends in ki. A content question ends in ki unless its ki- word is the first word of the sentence; a ki- word left where its answer would stand needs the final ki. The fronting exception covers ki-coda, ki-pago, ki-krono, ki-nela, ki-nela de, ki-kasa, ki-modo of manner and ki-muya-krono only. A ki- word in place in a clause without ki is an embedded question or part of a statement, never a direct question. Embedded questions never take ki; an embedded yes/no question uses ki-oro 2.5:G88 2.3:G77. A final ki belongs to the main clause and is the last word, but for a vocative set off by a comma 2.5:G89. A fragment question ends in ki too: Ata ki? And? 2.5:G91

Taivo lirala ki?
Do you sing?
Ki-pago taivo pagopa ki?
Where are you going?
Taivo pagopa ki-pago ki?
Where are you going?
Ki-kasa taivo po amora maivo?
Why did you love me?
Maivo ne sabesia ki-pago taivo futu pagopa.
I don’t know where you will go.
maivori ne sabesia ki-coda maivori ramura.
We do not know what we remember.

Earlier rulings on questions are kept as they were ruled, not deleted; 2.5:G88 amends 2.2:G60, and the 2.1 rulings it overtook were superseded in 2.2 2.5:G88 2.2:G61.

  • Exclamations. Only ki-modo + adjective (how …!), ki-eli + noun (what a …!) and ki-muya + noun (so many …!) make an exclamation, and only when the clause does not end in ki: Li nullo ka ki-modo vanisan! How beautiful the night is! Ki-eli nullo! What a night! Any other ki- clause without ki is a question, and a degree question must end in ki: Li nullo ka ki-modo vanisan ki? How beautiful is the night? 2.2:G61 So must a question with ki-eli or ki-muya + a noun, which without ki is an exclamation: Taivo amora ki-eli lira ki? Which song do you love? 2.3:G76
  • So … that. tonsi meaning “so” stands only before a predicate adjective, after a copula, and a fime clause after it gives the result: taivo ke tonsi corvisan fime taivo viki savalisia maivo ki? Are you so brave that you would kiss me? si maivo ke tonsi lukasan, maivo futu ne salrusia. If I am so lucky, I will not rise. Opening its clause, tonsi means “therefore” and takes a comma 2.5:G117.
  • Blessings and wishes. pifu with any subject is the blessing form: Li lumi tivrota taivo pifu. May the Light hold you. With noma, it is “if only”: Taivo po noma lisrola pifu. If only you had stayed. 2.1:G48
  • Commands and prohibitions. A command is marked: it ends in ! or contains fisa (in speech, fisa or command intonation); a subjectless, tenseless clause with neither is an elliptical statement. ne + a command forbids; yava ne + verb is “let us not”: Ne senobakosia maivo! Don’t interrupt me! Fisa, ne lirala. Please don’t sing. 2.5:G90 2.1:G56
