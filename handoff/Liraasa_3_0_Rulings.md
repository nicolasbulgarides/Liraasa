# Liraasa 3.0 Reference Grammar: every ruling

Verbatim from the Liraasa 3.0 lexicon, newest edition first. Ruling ids are edition-qualified: 0.8:G7 and 1.0:G7 are different rulings.

## Rulings 3.0

### 3.0:G129: The vocative particle iya

iya + noun phrase is a vocative: a direct address. It opens a sentence and is set off by a comma, stands alone as an invocation, or follows a final ki after a comma (2.5:G89). The noun phrase may be a name, a title, an epithet, a pronoun (taivo, taivori) or an owned phrase (maivo de amor). A vocative never changes the grammar of the clause it addresses. In everyday speech a bare name set off by commas remains a vocative (1.2:G31); in prayer, invocation and litany iya is expected, and it is required wherever a comma-set noun phrase could be read as an apposition (3.0:G130).

Refines 2.5:G89, 1.2:G31.

- *Iya Deviasa, lenila maivo!* — O Great Goddess, hear me!
- *Iya li mare de li lumi, maivo lirala vobi taivo.* — O mother of light, I sing for you.
- *Taivo viki pagopa ligo maivo ki, iya maivo de amor?* — Would you come near me, O my love?
- *Iya Lumimare!* — O Mother of Light!

### 3.0:G130: Apposition and epithets

A noun phrase may be followed by a second noun phrase, set off by commas, that renames it: an apposition. The apposed phrase keeps its own determiner, takes no copula, and is closed by a comma when the clause continues. Each phrase settles its own de-ownership: an apposition starts a new phrase, so it never joins a de-chain (2.5:G96). Several appositions in a row form a litany. A relative clause (raho) may end an apposition.

Refines 2.5:G96.

- *Li mareasa de li vemiasa, li una-ne lumi raho po kronobioka li veluri, po ke lavisan.* — The mother of the cosmos, the first light who gave birth to the stars, was glad.
- *Deviasa, li mare de li veluri, lirala.* — The Great Goddess, the mother of the stars, sings.
- *li lumi, li lumi de li veluri* — the light, the light of the stars
- *Iya Deviasa, Velumare, Lumimare, Liramare!* — O Great Goddess, Mother of the Stars, Mother of Light, Mother of Song!

### 3.0:G131: Divine names and titles

An ascended noun or title compound used as a name of the Goddess is capitalized and takes no article (Deviasa, Lumiasa, Mareasa, Velumare). Used as a name, it owns like a personal name when it stands before de (1.2:G27): Deviasa de lira is the Great Goddess’s song. An epithet that describes her keeps its article and stays lower-case: li mare de li lumi, the mother of light. Writing a descriptive epithet as a name therefore changes its meaning.

Refines 1.2:G27.

- *Deviasa de lira* — the Great Goddess’s song (a name owns)
- *li mareasa de li lumi* — the mother of light (an epithet: the light ‘owns’ the relation)
- *Lumimare lirala.* — The Mother of Light sings.

### 3.0:G132: Absolute being: SUBJECT + miyo

miyo with a subject before it and no noun phrase after it asserts that the subject exists: absolute being. It takes tense, negation and operators like any verb. miyo + noun phrase with no subject keeps its 2.5 meaning, ‘there is’. A copula (ke, ka) still always needs a predicate.

- *Saivo miyo.* — She is. (She exists.)
- *Maivo miyo.* — I am.
- *Saivo po miyo, saivo miyo, saivo futu miyo.* — She was, she is, she will be.
- *Kroti li pagoasa, Deviasa po miyo.* — Before the universe, the Great Goddess was.
- *Miyo baba sora en li nimu.* — There is a friend in the home. (2.5 meaning, unchanged)

### 3.0:G133: Fiat, blessing of existence, and the liturgical formulas

miyo + noun phrase marked as a command (‘!’) is a fiat: ‘let there be …’. miyo + noun phrase + pifu is a wish: ‘may there be …’. Unmarked, miyo + noun phrase stays a statement (2.5:G90). Two fixed liturgical formulas are canon: ‘Ola-modo po miyo, ola-modo miyo, ola-modo futu miyo’ (so it was, so it is, so it shall be), in which ola-modo opens an existential clause without a comma; and ‘Ola-modo pifu’ (so be it; amen), which closes a prayer.

Refines 2.5:G90.

- *Miyo lumi!* — Let there be light!
- *Miyo sove en li nimu pifu.* — May there be peace in the home.
- *Miyo lumi.* — There is light.
- *Ola-modo po miyo, ola-modo miyo, ola-modo futu miyo.* — So it was, so it is, so it shall be.
- *Ola-modo pifu.* — So be it. (Amen.)

### 3.0:G134: Emphatic sela

A pronoun or noun phrase + sela in subject position, or as an apposition, is emphatic: ‘herself’, ‘itself’. In object position, PRONOUN sela stays reflexive (unchanged). sela never stands alone as a subject.

- *Saivo sela po lirala.* — She herself sang.
- *Saivo po amora saivo sela.* — She loved herself. (reflexive)
- *Ito sela po ka li una-ne leninela.* — It itself was the first listener.
- *Saivo de nole po ka sambasan ko li melo, ito sela.* — Her name was the same as music, music itself.

### 3.0:G135: Commands, petitions and yava

A command may carry ne, the iteration words reve and loki, and any adjuncts; it never carries tense, a modal or an evidential. A petition to the divine is a command opened by a vocative (3.0:G129), usually softened by fisa after the vocative’s comma. yava may follow a FRAME.

- *Iya Deviasa, fisa, ne pagopa vofa maivo!* — O Great Goddess, please, do not go far from me!
- *Reve lirala!* — Sing again!
- *Eli-krono, yava lirala!* — Now, let us sing!
- *Iya Lumimare, fisa, lenila maivo de lirajuba.* — O Mother of Light, please hear my song-offering.

### 3.0:G136: Authorship: gagu after a work

After a noun naming a made work (lira, lavbulira, lirasarbi, telne, sarbiasa …), gagu + noun phrase names its maker, without a passive verb. Elsewhere gagu remains the agent of gome (2.5:G110).

Refines 2.5:G110.

- *li lira gagu Peta* — the song by Peta
- *Liraasa, li lira de li una-ne veluri gagu li una-ne mareasa* — Liraasa, the song of the first stars, by the first mother

### 3.0:G137: Acclamations

A verbless acclamation NP ala NP! (‘NP to NP!’) is grammatical as a liturgical fragment: glory, praise or thanks offered to someone. It may be followed by a vocative.

- *Lumisirmo ala Deviasa!* — Glory to the Great Goddess!
- *Benaseno ala taivo, iya Lumimare!* — Praise to you, O Mother of Light!
- *Lorejuba ala taivo, iya Velumare.* — Thanks to you, O Mother of the Stars.

### 3.0:G138: Divine pronouns (orthography)

Taivo, Saivo and their forms may be capitalized mid-sentence when they refer to the Goddess. This is an optional convention of written devotion and has no grammatical effect.

- *Maivo amora Taivo.* — I love You.
- *Saivo miyo, ata Saivo de lumi lumila.* — She is, and Her light shines.

### 3.0:G139: For ever: en rinbakrono

The new noun rinbakrono (eternity) is a time noun: as ‘when’ it takes en (2.5:G103). en rinbakrono means ‘for ever; in eternity’. toda-krono (always) is unchanged. Durations in general (‘for two days’) remain an open question.

Refines 2.5:G103.

- *Saivo futu lirala en rinbakrono.* — She will sing for ever.
- *Maivo amora taivo toda-krono.* — I love you always.

### 3.0:G140: Distributive succession: X krotiapo X

A bare noun or number repeated around krotiapo (‘X after X’) is a distributive adjunct or FRAME meaning ‘one X after another’: una krotiapo una, one by one; velu krotiapo velu, star after star. It extends kroti/krotiapo + a bare noun (2.3:G79).

Refines 2.3:G79.

- *Una krotiapo una, li veluri po hiro lirala.* — One by one, the stars began to sing.
- *Velu krotiapo velu, li navu po loya lumisan.* — Star after star, the sky became bright.

### 3.0:G141: Register and restraint (style, non-normative)

Ascended forms are most powerful against ordinary ones. Keep ordinary forms for the human and the near; reserve ascended forms for what is truly divine; let a change of register mark a change of nearness (a mortal growing toward the divine, or the Goddess stooping to comfort). This is guidance for composition, not grammar.

- *Maivo ke nemesan. Taivo ka loreasa.* — I am sorrowful. You are a heart larger than sorrow.

## Rulings 2.5

### 2.5:G87: The album is not grammar canon

The album 'Liraasa: Songs of the First Stars' is not grammar canon. It stays in the data as text, marked non-normative. No album line licenses a construction, and album_reading annotations no longer grant exceptions.

Supersedes 2.2:G68.

### 2.5:G88: Questions end in ki; a fronted ki-word may drop it

A yes/no question ends in ki. A content question ends in ki unless its ki-word is the first word of the sentence; a ki-word left in its answer's place requires the final ki. This exception covers ki-coda, ki-pago, ki-krono, ki-nela, ki-nela de, ki-kasa, manner ki-modo and ki-muya-krono only. Questions with ki-eli + noun, ki-muya + noun and degree ki-modo + adjective always end in ki (2.3:G76, 2.2:G61 stand), and the same three without ki remain exclamations. Embedded questions never take ki (2.3:G77 stands). A ki-word in its answer's place in a clause without final ki is an embedded question or part of a statement, never a direct question.

Refines 2.2:G60.

- *Ki-kasa taivo po amora maivo?* — Why did you love me?
- *Ki-kasa taivo po amora maivo ki?* — Why did you love me?
- *Taivo pagopa ki-pago ki?* — Where are you going?
- *Maivo po masuma li talirivo ki-kasa.* — I asked my colleague why. (statement)
- *Ki-eli nullo!* — What a night! (exclamation)

### 2.5:G89: Which clause a final ki or attitude word belongs to

A ki or attitude word (seye, mufe, poye, pifu, zoke) at the end of a sentence belongs to the main clause, even when a subordinate clause follows it. Doubt inside a trailing subordinate clause is expressed with a modal (nosi); an embedded yes/no question uses ki-oro. ki is the last word of the sentence, except that a vocative set off by a comma may follow it.

Refines 1.0:G22.

- *Taivo sabesia fime maivo lirala poye.* — Perhaps you know that I sing.
- *Taivo sabesia fime maivo nosi lirala.* — You know that I might sing.
- *Taivo sabesia fime maivo lirala ki?* — Do you know that I sing?

### 2.5:G126: The clause's closing order and its edge positions

A clause closes in this order: … VERB [OBJECT] [ADJUNCTS] [fuyi] [STATUS: hoyo | yasmo] [ATTITUDE] [ki]. Closing time words, durations and clause-final reve / loki stand among the adjuncts, before fuyi. Outside the core slots, a clause has edge positions. Before it, in order: a line linker (ata, oro, nara), FRAME, TOPIC, then a fronted ki-word (2.5:G88). After it: a vocative set off by a comma, which may follow ki.

Refines 2.3:G78, 2.1:G55.

- *Maivori lirala toda-krono fuyi pifu.* — May we always sing to each other.
- *Ki-pago taivo pagopa?* — Where are you going? (fronted ki-word)
- *Taivo viki pagopa ligo maivo ki, maivo de amor?* — Would you come near me, my love?

### 2.5:G127: What may stand in FRAME

FRAME, the clause-opening slot set off by a comma, holds a time adverbial or a place adverbial. Tense words (po, futu, pressa) and iteration words (reve, loki, bodu) are not frame words and are never closing time words.

Refines 1.2:G24.

- *Eli-pago, maivo lirala.* — Here, I sing.
- *Deyaapo, maivo po lirala.* — Yesterday, I sang.

### 2.5:G90: A command is marked

A command is marked. In writing it ends in '!' or contains fisa; in speech, fisa or command intonation marks it. A subjectless, tenseless clause with neither mark is an elliptical statement, not a command.

Refines 1.2:G31, 2.1:G56.

- *Ne lirala!* — Don't sing!
- *Fisa, ne lirala.* — Please don't sing.
- *Fisa, lisrola.* — Please stay.
- *Taivo lirala ki? — Ne lirala.* — Do you sing? — (I) don't sing.

### 2.5:G91: Fragment questions

A fragment question follows the ki rule: it ends in ki.

- *Ata ki?* — And?

### 2.5:G92: Echo ki

Echo ki attaches only to a single word or a bare noun phrase. raho + clause + ki is therefore always a direct question (2.3:G74 stands).

Refines 0.8:G3.

- *Raho lirala ki?* — Who sings?
- *Ki-nela lirala ki?* — Who sings?
- *Lira ki?* — A song? (echo)

### 2.5:G93: ke and ka: one contrast for every predicate

ke and ka mark one contrast for every kind of predicate (adjective, noun phrase or location): ke = holds now, or for a time; ka = lasting, defining, by nature.

Supersedes clause.copulas definitions of ke and ka, function_words rows ke, ka (definitions).

- *Li kavbikova ke su li tabu.* — The basket is on the table (right now).
- *Li nimu ka ligo li cilni.* — The home is near the city (it always has been).
- *Maivo po ke linuti.* — I was a little child (back then).
- *Maivo ka lirayo.* — I am a singer (it's who I am).
- *Li nela ke benasan.* — The person is good at the moment.
- *Li sora ka benasan.* — The friend is good by nature.

### 2.5:G128: The minimal clause

The minimal clause is SUBJECT + VERB, with these exceptions: commands, hortatives, permitted ellipsis, existential miyo clauses (which have no subject), and bare adjective predicates. A bare adjective predicate (SUBJECT ADJECTIVE, no copula) is allowed only in the present with no operators; anything more needs ke or ka.

- *Nela vanisan.* — The person is beautiful.
- *Li nela po ke vanisan.* — The person was beautiful.
- *Miyo baba sora en li nimu.* — There is a friend in the home.

### 2.5:G94: po ka and kapoki

po ka = was (lasting), with no claim about now. kapoki = was, and no longer is. (With 2.5:G93, po ke = was, at that time.)

- *Saivo po ka benasan.* — They were good (no claim about now).
- *Saivo kapoki benasan.* — They were good, and no longer are.

### 2.5:G95: Kinship nouns are not names for possession

Kinship nouns (pilo, mare and the rest) are never treated as names for possession. Only a pronoun, or a personal name standing before de, is the owner; otherwise X de Y = 'X of Y' (Y owns). When both sides are personal names, the left one owns.

Refines 1.2:G27, 1.2:G32.

- *li bumre de pilo* — father's bread
- *pilo de bumre* — the father of the bread
- *Kipo de Dorin* — Kipo's Dorin
- *maivo de lira* — my song

### 2.5:G96: Chains of de

Chains of de group from the left: X de Y de Z = (X de Y) de Z. The ownership rules apply first to X de Y, then to that whole phrase de Z.

- *li suma de maivo de yuge* — my warmth, of the body  [(li suma de maivo) de yuge]

### 2.5:G97: NUMBER de NUMBER

NUMBER de NUMBER may be a fraction or a partitive; context decides. Fractions are written with spaces (tressa de cuara), never hyphenated.

- *tressa de cuara* — three quarters
- *Una de dossa futu relnura.* — One of the two will return / One half will return.

### 2.5:G98: Noun-phrase order

Noun-phrase order: [QUANTIFIER] [DETERMINER: li | baba | eli | ola | eliri | olari | ki-eli | OWNER de] [ORDINAL] [NUMBER] {[DEGREE WORD] ADJECTIVE} NOUN[-ti][-ri] [de NP] [raho CLAUSE]. The ordinal stands right after the determiner, before the adjectives. Degree words (ruli, kasi, menu, mosa, lusu, gase, diba) stand directly before the adjective they modify. ki-muya fills the QUANTIFIER slot. OWNER de takes only a pronoun or a personal name (2.5:G95).

Supersedes clause.noun_phrase template.

- *li una-ne ruli vanisan lira* — the first very beautiful song
- *baba ruli vanisan lira* — a very beautiful song
- *eliri lirari* — these songs

### 2.5:G99: li after a side noun

li may not be dropped after a side noun (nazru, zavme, silda, cemnu, lizma, rizno) used as a preposition.

- *Li lavra semnesia nazru li nimu.* — The bird sits behind the home.

### 2.5:G100: maivo: I and Self

maivo is deliberately both the pronoun 'I' and the noun 'Self'. Context decides.

- *Maivo ka vanisan.* — I am beautiful. / The Self is beautiful.

### 2.5:G101: Which words take -ri, and plural agreement

-ri attaches to nouns, pronouns, demonstratives, and an adjective used as a noun after a determiner; never to a bare adjective or a verb. After any count word (a number, a quantifier, eliri or olari), -ri on the noun is optional, chosen by sound and meter. A singular demonstrative (eli, ola) takes a singular noun.

Refines 1.2:G28.

- *li benasanri* — the good ones
- *eliri lira* — these songs
- *eliri lirari* — these songs
- *dossa lira* — two songs

### 2.5:G102: Pronouns for animals

An animal is referred to with ito by default. A named animal takes saivo.

- *Li lavra lirala; ito ka vanisan.* — The bird sings; it is beautiful.
- *Nilo lirala; saivo ka vanisan.* — Nilo (the bird) sings; she is beautiful.

### 2.5:G103: Time nouns as "when"

Only listed time adverbials stand bare as 'when': entries in the time-adverbial table, -krono correlatives, and day compounds (deyapressa, deyaapo, deyafutu). A plain time noun (merno, nullo, vesno, mambi, lornu …) used as 'when' takes en. A bare plain noun after a verb is its object.

- *Maivo vimova en merno.* — I see in the morning.
- *Maivo vimova merno.* — I see the morning.
- *Maivo lirala deyapressa.* — I sing today.
- *Maivo lirala toda-krono.* — I always sing.

### 2.5:G104: yuta, en, ko and la: one test per word

One test per word. yuta = a tool, means or substance handled or used up. en = a medium or state one is in, including a manner seen as a medium. ko = a companion, or an emotion that accompanies the act. la + adjective = the quality of the action.

Refines 1.2:G33, 2.3:G73.

- *Maivo lallela li dorbi yuta li kemye.* — I open the door with the key.
- *Saivo po senosia en baba melosan valla.* — She spoke in a musical voice.
- *Maivo lirala ko amor.* — I sing with love.
- *Maivo lirala ko saivo.* — I sing with him.
- *Maivo lirala la molusan.* — I sing gently.

### 2.5:G105: Where la + adjective stands

la + adjective stands only among the adjuncts, after the verb and object. A degree word goes between la and the adjective: la ruli molusan.

- *Maivo lirala la ruli molusan.* — I sing very gently.

### 2.5:G106: Comparing amounts and manner

Amounts: kasi or menu + noun … tamo NP. Manner: la kasi (or la menu) + adjective … tamo NP.

- *Maivo tovota kasi lirari tamo taivo.* — I have more songs than you.
- *Maivo lirala la kasi molusan tamo taivo.* — I sing more gently than you.

### 2.5:G107: gase + a number

gase + a number may mean 'about' or 'like'; context decides.

- *Maivo tovota gase dossa cono.* — I have about two coins. / … something like two coins.

### 2.5:G108: Measures with kroti and krotiapo

A measure (for kroti 'ago' and krotiapo 'from now') is NUMBER + unit, or a listed quantifier + unit. Listed quantifiers: AUTHOR INPUT NEEDED.

- *Maivo po venreva kroti dossa deya.* — I came two days ago.

### 2.5:G109: Valency operators: the full pattern

Valency operators vaku (make), lesvu (let) and tekvu (prevent): CAUSER [TENSE] [noma] [ne] [MODAL] … [EVIDENTIAL] OP ACTOR [ne] [APPROACH] [PHASE] [EFFORT] VERB [OBJECT]. Tense, modal and evidential belong to the main clause; the actor's own negation, approach, phase and effort follow the actor. The embedded verb may take an object.

Refines 1.0:G14.

- *Maivo vaku saivo sapusia li nami.* — I make him eat the food.
- *Taivo vaku maivo lirala.* — You make me sing.
- *Maivo po lesvu saivo pagopa.* — I let them leave.

### 2.5:G110: Passives, and change-of-state verbs

gome may passivise any verb that is attested with an object anywhere in the data. Without gome, the subject is the doer, except for the listed change-of-state verbs, whose subject may be the thing that changes (an anticausative reading).

- *Li lira gome sarbisia gagu maivo.* — The song is written by me.
- *Zafra, li dorbi po lallela.* — Suddenly the door opened.
- *Zafra, li dorbi po gome lallela.* — Suddenly the door was opened.

### 2.5:G111: Adjective + sidu + verb: no dummy ito

In ADJECTIVE + sidu + VERB, the thing the adjective describes is the subject of the clause and the understood object of the verb. There is no dummy ito.

Refines 2.2:G65.

- *Trana ke ruli levasan sidu zeldoza.* — Change is very easy to resist.

### 2.5:G112: A bare pronoun recipient

A pronoun recipient may stand bare right after any verb whose meaning has a recipient (giving, lending, sending, owing, paying, bringing, teaching, showing, telling, singing to …). Otherwise, and always for a non-pronoun recipient, the recipient takes ala after the theme.

Refines 1.2:G26.

- *Taivo guraga maivo dossa cono.* — You owe me two coins.
- *Maivo lirala taivo baba lira.* — I sing you a song.
- *Maivo lirala baba lira ala taivo.* — I sing a song to you.

### 2.5:G113: Purpose always uses kopisa

Purpose always uses kopisa. With the same subject, kopisa + bare verb phrase (no subject); with a different subject, kopisa + clause. sidu stays for verb chains (desida sidu lirala).

- *Maivo po venreva eli-pago kopisa lirala.* — I came here to sing.
- *Maivo lirala kopisa taivo rilura.* — I sing so that you dance.

### 2.5:G114: "Would have" is po noma viki

'Would have' is always po noma viki. po viki without noma is not a form.

Refines 1.0:G6.

- *Si maivo po noma masuma, taivo po noma viki relsira.* — If I had asked, you would have answered.

### 2.5:G115: The comma that closes a raho clause

A raho clause that does not end the sentence is closed by a comma (a pause in speech) when it contains its own object or adjunct. A raho clause of only a verb (and its operators) needs no comma.

Refines 1.0:G21.

- *Li nela raho lirala ka benasan.* — The person who sings is good.
- *Li sabeyo raho vismava eli, ne yehu vimova li vordu.* — The sage who studies this did not witness the word.

### 2.5:G116: Hopeful purpose: kopisa + nosi

Hopeful purpose ('so that perhaps', 'in the hope that') is kopisa + a clause whose verb carries nosi. fime always introduces a content clause ('that').

Supersedes 2.2:G63.

- *Maivo lirala, kopisa taivo nosi rilura.* — I sing, so that perhaps you'll dance.
- *Maivo sabesia fime taivo nosi lirala.* — I know that you might sing.

### 2.5:G117: tonsi: therefore, and so

Clause-initial tonsi means 'therefore' and is followed by a comma. tonsi meaning 'so' (degree) stands only before a predicate adjective, after a copula.

Refines 2.2:G62.

- *Tonsi, corvisan nela lirala.* — Therefore a brave person sings.
- *Taivo ke tonsi corvisan fime taivo viki savalisia maivo ki?* — Are you so brave that you would kiss me?

### 2.5:G118: Tense in subordinate clauses

An unmarked subordinate clause takes the main clause's tense, whatever its linker. sige clauses are the exception and always mark their own tense. A subordinate clause with its own tense word keeps it.

Refines 0.8:R6.

- *Kureli taivo venreva, maivo po lirala.* — As soon as you came, I sang.
- *Maivo po masuma ki-oro taivo relnura.* — I asked whether you returned.

### 2.5:G119: Picking up a hozi topic

A topic that is the subject of its clause must be picked up by a pronoun. A non-subject topic may be picked up or left as a gap; a gap is read as the verb's object.

- *Kipo hozi, saivo lirala.* — As for Kipo, they sing.
- *Li bumre hozi, Kipo po bumreba.* — As for the bread, Kipo baked it.
- *Li bumre hozi, Kipo po bumreba ito.* — As for the bread, Kipo baked it.

### 2.5:G120: Relatives on a possessor, a time or a place

Relative clauses on a possessor, a time or a place use a resumptive pronoun in place, as 2.1:G52 does for prepositions: possessor → PRONOUN de NOUN; time or place → the needed preposition + ito.

Refines 2.1:G52.

- *li lirayo raho maivo amora saivo de lira* — the singer whose song I love
- *li krono raho maivo po lirala en ito* — the time when I sang
- *li pago raho maivo po venreva da ito* — the place I came from

### 2.5:G121: The motion prefixes are productive

The motion prefixes are productive on any verb, with compositional meanings: va- = toward the speaker, here; du- = away, giving away; taro- = across, through; yave- = together, to meet; revo- = back, again. A listed meaning (the four curated phrases and any listed prefixed verb) overrides the compositional one. 'Motion verb' is no longer a category the grammar needs. lu-, nu-, mi-, so- stay retired.

Supersedes the closed-list part of 1.2:G35, settled_questions[6], word_building.motion_prefixes.rule.

- *va-lirala* — come toward (me) while singing
- *revo-lirala* — sing back, sing again
- *du-vimova* — look away
- *taro-pagopa* — go across
- *Maivo revo-sabesia taivo.* — I recognize you. (listed meaning)

### 2.5:G122: -ti is an inflection

-ti is an inflection-like suffix, not one of the derivations. Word ::= ROOT + (one form OR one derivation) + -ti + -ri; -ti may follow any form or derivation.

Refines 2.0:G41.

- *lirayoti* — little singer
- *lirati* — little song
- *liratiri* — little songs

### 2.5:G123: Words ending in pago

A word ending in pago is the same word whether read as ROOT + -pago or as a compound with the root pago. It is a noun with no further forms unless its dictionary line lists them.

Refines 2.1:G59.

- *lemupago* — school

### 2.5:G124: Image compounds have all seven forms

Image compounds have all seven forms, built by the ordinary form rules, with predictable meanings. This is an explicit exception to hard_rules[1] ('only listed forms exist'), which still governs every other headword.

- *lunosavali* — moonlit kiss
- *lunosavalila* — to kiss by moonlight
- *lunosavalisan* — like a moonlit kiss

### 2.5:G125: Negative numbers: antizi

Negative numbers: antizi + NUMBER (antizi dossa = minus two). antizi is a new function word.

- *antizi dossa* — minus two

### 2.3:G69: Second respelling pass

Thirteen roots are respelled: lenra -> zarku; vanru -> dokra; voho -> kezdo; vabale -> tobra; teyo -> dento; mege -> yumli; kupa -> ruko; jemla -> zimzi; pesbi -> bondo; menha -> gamdo; vugo -> vizu; salye -> bapi; senba -> linle. 2.1:G58 applies in full: a respelled root keeps every meaning and empty slot; all forms, derivations, compounds and image compounds respell with it; compound verbs follow the new first letter; 2.2 spellings retired; older texts, including the album, are read under the spellings they were written in.

Refines 2.1:G58.

### 2.3:G70: Roots for common animals

bumbo (bear), murmu (cow; cattle) and kerku (frog) are new roots. They replace larbopagoregna, maremirulazu and the listed derivation mirunululirayo, which are retired; kerku takes the verb kerkuka, to croak. mirunululira (river-song) stays.

### 2.3:G71: Wolf

larbopagobudi -> farvibudi (farvi + budi), 'wild dog', read like farvilazu (wild animal); lunolarbopagobudi -> lunofarvibudi. Supersedes the wolf half of 2.1:G59; its taha -> kezupago half stands.

Supersedes 2.1:G59.

### 2.3:G72: A line that continues: ata

ata may open a line or clause that continues the one before, as oro may (1.2:G25): 'And …'. The album opens lines with ata throughout (2.2:G68).

Refines 1.2:G25, 1.0:G13.

- *ata maivo lemula saivo de sizosan lumi.* — And I learn his shimmering light.
- *ata maivori revo-lirala ata relsira!* — And we sing again and answer!

### 2.3:G73: Manner with en and ko

en + an abstract noun names the manner or medium of an act: en baba melosan valla, in a musical voice; en veraasa, in truth. ko + a feeling names the spirit in which it is done: ko amor, with love; ko bapi, in surprise. yuta stays the tool or means (1.2:G33); with an abstract noun, choose by meaning. As the album sings (2.2:G68).

Refines 1.2:G33.

- *saivo po senosia en baba melosan valla.* — She spoke in a musical voice.
- *Maivo lirala ko amor.* — And the stars wrote in surprise on the strength of phoenix-like love.

### 2.3:G74: raho opening a question

A direct question may open with raho for 'who' or 'what' and ends in ki, as the album asks twice (05:22, 11:12). ki-nela and ki-coda remain the ordinary question words. Supersedes the clause of 2.2:G64 that raho is never a question word; headless raho stands.

Supersedes 2.2:G64.

- *raho povi sabesia baba kasi polvasan sove ki?* — Who can know a more powerful peace?

### 2.3:G75: pagopa takes the motion prefixes

pagopa (to go) is a motion verb: the motion prefixes attach to it, as in taro-pagopa, to go across (album 05alt). The prefixes stay a closed list, on the motion verbs and the curated metaphors only (1.2:G35).

Refines 1.2:G35.

- *taivo ne taro-pagopa, nara lalfola hupi li lelde,* — you do not go across, but float above the edge,

### 2.3:G76: Questions with ki-eli and ki-muya

A question with ki-eli + noun or ki-muya + noun ends in ki; without ki the clause is an exclamation (2.2:G61). Other ki-word questions may still end without ki (2.2:G60).

Refines 2.2:G60, 2.2:G61.

- *Taivo amora ki-eli lira ki?* — Which song do you love?

### 2.3:G77: Embedded yes/no questions

An embedded yes/no question is opened by ki-oro, never by si, which means only 'if' (1.0:G2, 2.2:G60). Supersedes the clause of 0.8:G4 that has si mean 'whether'.

Supersedes 0.8:G4.

- *Maivo masuma ki-oro taivo futu relnura.* — I ask whether you will return.

### 2.3:G78: Opening and closing a clause

nara may open a line that continues the one before, as ata and oro may (2.3:G72, 1.2:G25). Hortative yava stands where the subject would, opening the clause. A frame may hold more than one time phrase, the wider first. A clause closes in this order: a closing time word, an attitude word, ki (1.2:G24, 1.0:G22).

Refines 1.2:G24, 1.0:G22, 2.1:G56.

- *Nara li devi po ne lenila.* — But the goddess did not listen.
- *Yava ne lirala.* — Let us not sing.
- *Saivori de lira sasvasia taivo toda-krono pifu.* — May their song protect you always.

### 2.3:G79: Time phrases

A measure is a number or a quantifier with a unit: kroti + a measure is 'ago' (kroti dossa deya, two days ago), and kroti + a bare noun is 'before' it (kroti deya) (2.1:G55). 'Since' a time is da + the time word: da ola-krono, since then. tuni may take a noun phrase of time: tuni merno, until morning.

Refines 2.1:G55.

- *Maivo po venreva kroti dossa deya.* — I came two days ago.
- *Da ola-krono, maivori lirala fuyi.* — Since then, we sing to each other.
- *Li lirayo po sulnosia tuni merno.* — The singer waited until morning.

### 2.3:G80: About, after hearing and knowing

1.2:G30 extends to verbs of hearing, knowing and thinking: give the verb an object with a de phrase. Saivo po lenila li telne de li lirayo, he heard about the singer (the story of the singer).

Refines 1.2:G30.

- *Li tarnuregna po lenila li telne de li lirayo.* — The king heard about the singer.

### 2.3:G81: lusu and mosa

Before a number or quantity, lusu and mosa are 'at least' and 'at most' (1.0:G9); before an adjective they make the superlative: mosa vanisan, most beautiful; lusu vanisan, least beautiful.

Refines 1.0:G9.

- *Taivo guraga maivo lusu dossa cono.* — You owe me at least two coins.
- *Taivo de lira ka mosa vanisan.* — Your song is the most beautiful.

### 2.3:G82: An adjective noun keeps its article

An adjective standing as a noun keeps its determiner even where li could otherwise be omitted (0.8:R5, 1.2:G29): li corvisan, the brave one.

Refines 1.2:G29, 0.8:R5.

- *Li corvisan lirala.* — The brave one sings.

### 2.3:G83: Quotations and past exclamations

A direct quotation follows its verb of saying after a colon, in quotation marks; ki may close the main clause before it (0.8:G4). An exclamation in the past uses ki-modo + adjective with a copula and its tense (2.2:G61).

Refines 0.8:G4, 2.2:G61.

- *Li devi po masuma: “Ki-nela roni lirala ki?”* — The goddess asked: “Who is singing?”
- *Li nullo po ka ki-modo vanisan!* — How beautiful the night was!

### 2.3:G84: Superseded rulings

A superseded ruling is kept as it was ruled. A construction it allowed stays grammatical in texts written under it and is not wrong in new text, but the superseding ruling is the one to follow.

### 2.3:G85: A retired listed derivation

A listed derivation retired for a headword stays buildable with its default reading (2.0:G41); a text written before the retirement reads it through the legacy table, as mirunululirayo reads kerku (2.3:G70).

Refines 2.0:G41, 2.3:G70.

### 2.3:G86: Ascended forms and the sacred

Whether an ascended form (-asa, -asasan) is sacred or simply the highest degree of its root depends on the word and its gloss; a sacred text uses the sacred ones (0.8:R10).

Refines 0.8:R10.

## Rulings 2.2

### 2.2:G60: Question-word position

A ki-word may open the clause or stay where its answer would stand; the album fronts it in most questions. A yes/no question must end in ki. A question with a ki-word may end in ki or not. Embedded wh-questions may also front the ki-word and never end in ki; embedded yes/no questions use ki-oro (1.0:G2).

Supersedes 2.1:G46, 0.8:G4.

- *Ki-pago taivo pagopa ki?*
- *Taivo pagopa ki-pago ki?*
- *Ki-kasa taivo po amora maivo?*
- *maivori ne sabesia ki-coda maivori ramura.*

### 2.2:G61: Exclamatives, narrowed

Only ki-modo + adjective, ki-eli + noun and ki-muya + noun make an exclamation, and only when the clause does not end in ki. Any other ki-word clause without ki is a question. A degree question (ki-modo + adjective) must end in ki.

Supersedes 2.1:G47.

- *Li nullo ka ki-modo vanisan!*
- *Ki-eli nullo!*
- *Li nullo ka ki-modo vanisan ki?*

### 2.2:G62: So … that: tonsi + adjective

tonsi directly before an adjective means “so”; a following fime clause gives the result. Clause-initial tonsi keeps its meaning “therefore”.

Supersedes 2.1:G54.

- *taivo ke tonsi corvisan fime taivo viki savalisia maivo ki?*
- *si maivo ke tonsi lukasan, maivo futu ne salrusia.*

### 2.2:G63: Hopeful purpose: fime + nosi

**Superseded by 2.5:G116.** Kept as it was ruled; follow 2.5:G116.

After a main clause, a fime clause whose verb carries nosi means “in the hope that, so that perhaps”. kopisa stays the plain purpose linker.

- *maivo futu donuda ito biti de lalfo, fime taivo nosi semnesia ko maivo en moluvaye.*

### 2.2:G64: Headless raho

**In part superseded by 2.3:G74:** its clause “raho is never a question word” is replaced; the rest stands.

raho with no noun before it means “the one who, that which”, as subject, object or complement. raho is never a question word: questions use ki-nela or ki-coda.

- *Maivo lirala raho taivo amora.*
- *saivo po farufasbifa kube saivo ramura raho maivo po ke.*

### 2.2:G65: Adjective + sidu + verb

An adjective may take sidu + verb for “easy to …, ready to …”. The verb’s object is what the adjective describes.

- *ito ke ruli levasan sidu zeldoza trana.*

### 2.2:G66: kroti and krotiapo before a clause

kroti and krotiapo may introduce a clause: “before …”, “after …”. The clause inherits tense like kube (0.8:R6). Before a measure they keep 2.1:G55; before li + noun, their 2.0 meaning.

- *kroti maivo lesvu taivo ke li sevusan ruhe.*

### 2.2:G67: Side nouns as prepositions

nazru (behind), zavme (in front of), silda (beside), cemnu (in the middle of), lizma (left of) and rizno (right of) may stand bare before a noun phrase as prepositions, or after a verb as adverbs.

- *Li lavra semnesia nazru li nimu.*
- *saivo de melonulu venreva nazru gase baba lira de ruhesonbu.*

### 2.2:G68: The album as reference corpus

**Superseded by 2.5:G87.** Kept as it was ruled; follow 2.5:G87.

The album (twelve songs and the alternate 05) is canon 2.0 text, read with 2.0 spellings under 2.1:G58. A construction it uses consistently is grammar. A line that departs from the grammar once is an album reading: it keeps its intended meaning, is listed with its standard form, and is not a model for new text.

## Rulings 2.1

### 2.1:G46: Questions keep every word in place

**Superseded by 2.2:G60.** Kept as it was ruled; follow 2.2:G60.

A question word stands where its answer would stand; every direct question ends in ki. A ki-word of time may open the clause as a frame. Embedded questions unchanged (0.8:G4, 1.0:G2). Replaces the fronted small-words examples: Taivo pagopa ki-pago ki?

Refines 1.0:G22.

### 2.1:G47: Exclamatives

**Superseded by 2.2:G61.** Kept as it was ruled; follow 2.2:G61.

A main clause with a ki-word and no final ki is an exclamation (!). ki-modo + ADJ = how ...!; ki-eli + N = what a ...!; ki-muya + N = so many ...! Li nullo ka ki-modo vanisan!

### 2.1:G48: Blessings and wishes

pifu with any subject is the blessing form: Li lumi tivrota taivo pifu. noma + pifu = if only: Taivo po noma lisrola pifu.

### 2.1:G49: Once and N times

NUMBER-krono = N times (una-krono once, dossa-krono twice); ki-muya-krono = how many times; una-ne krono = the first time; una-krono never means formerly (somi-krono).

A second verb with its own subject goes in a fime clause: Maivo desida fime taivo lisrola. sidu stays same-subject; fime inherits tense (0.8:R6).

Refines 0.8:R6.

### 2.1:G51: Evidentials

No evidential = no claim about source. Inside fime, the evidential reports the main-clause subject's evidence. In a question it asks the listener's source.

### 2.1:G52: Relatives on a prepositional object

raho opens the clause; the preposition keeps its place with a resumptive pronoun (ito/itori, saivo/saivori): Li nimu raho maivo po lirala en ito, ka vanisan.

### 2.1:G53: Partitive de

After a number, quantifier, or superlative, de means of/among and G27/G32 ownership does not apply: Una de maivori futu relnura. Fractions are the same construction.

### 2.1:G54: Result clauses

**Superseded by 2.2:G62.** Kept as it was ruled; follow 2.2:G62.

ola-modo + ADJ ... fime CLAUSE = so ADJ that: Taivo ke ola-modo vanisan fime li veluri lasola.

### 2.1:G55: Duration, ago, from now

Bare measure after the object = duration; kroti + measure (no article) = ago; krotiapo + measure = in/after. kroti/krotiapo + li + noun = before/after it.

### 2.1:G56: Prohibitions

ne + command, no subject; fisa softens; yava ne + verb = let us not.

Guidance for new and respelled roots: prefer CVCV; l m n r v for sacred/tender/lyrical; b d g k p t z h for bodily/everyday/harsh; playful b c p for beloved small creatures; related meanings may share a consonant frame; no suffix-look-alike endings (-ti -ri -na -vu -yo -asa); at least two letters from every root and small word except deliberate families. Not retroactive.

A respelled root keeps every meaning and empty slot; all forms, derivations, compounds and image compounds respell with it; compound verbs follow the new first letter; 2.0 spellings retired, 2.0 texts read under 2.0 spellings; overrides protected for these 30 roots; new spellings protected.

## Rulings 2.0

### 2.0:G37: Two declared one-edit pairs

Declare devo / devi and nevi / devi as permitted one-edit pairs.

### 2.0:G38: Colour bases in compounds; compounds lexicalised with -ti

Colour bases may act as the modifier in a dictionary compound, and a compound may be lexicalised with -ti.

### 2.0:G39: Gold and silver as headwords

aurumijuba and vanajuba become dictionary headwords as well as colour words.

### 2.0:G40: Image compounds

Image compounds: any listed modifier + any listed head is a valid word with a predictable meaning, no 'new' flag needed (see pattern_compounds).

### 2.0:G41: Productive derivations

Derivations are fully productive with default readings: R+yo = one who does R, or one devoted to R; R+vu = a big or intense R; R+pago = the place of R; R+ti = a little or dear R. They attach to any root or compound (as lemudonuyo and mambicuzayo already do). Listed derivations keep their fixed meanings and override the default.

### 2.0:G42: Nested compounds

A compound may take a compound as one of its two parts: MODIFIER + HEAD still joins exactly two headwords, either of which may itself be a compound (larbopago + safa = larbopagosafa). 1.2 avoided nesting; 2.0 allows it.

### 2.0:G43: Consonant values

Vowels are pure, as in Spanish or Italian. l m n s v b d p t f k sound as in English; y as in "yes"; r is a single tap, as in Spanish "pero"; g is always hard, as in "go"; h as in "hope"; z as in "zone". c is always "ch" as in "chant", before every vowel: ca = cha, co = cho, and likewise ce, ci, cu. j is the voiced partner of c, as in "jewel". sh is as in "shade"; ch, written only in a few colour and number words, is the same sound as c.

### 2.0:G44: Neighbouring vowels in song

Every written vowel is its own syllable (0.8:R8), but in singing neighbouring vowels may glide into one note: benaasa may be sung be-na-a-sa or be-naa-sa. The spelling never changes; the songwriter chooses the count the melody needs.

Refines 0.8:R8.

### 2.0:G45: Foreign names

A foreign name is not translated. It is copied as closely as Liraasa letters allow, choosing among near spellings by how the name sounds and sings.

## Rulings 1.2

### 1.2:G24: Time words: opening or closing

A time word or time correlative (eli-krono, deyapressa, fututi, ki-krono's answers…) may open its clause or close it. When it opens the clause, the comma after it is optional. Only the four frame words zafra, finra, lanto and kenvi are opening-only, and they keep their comma.

Refines 1.0 slot 1 FRAME.

- *Eli-krono, maivo lirala.* — Now I sing.
- *Eli-krono maivo lirala.* — Now I sing.
- *Saivo po venreva pendi eli-krono.* — They arrived exactly now.
- *Zafra, li dorbi po lallela.* — Suddenly the door opened.

### 1.2:G25: oro, alone and paired

A single oro joins two equal constituents, exactly as ata does: miru oro nami, water or food. It may also open a clause or line that continues the one before ("Or …"). The pair oro A oro B is the emphatic "either A or B".

Refines 1.0:G10.

- *miru oro nami* — water or food
- *oro maivo ke li suma ki?* — Or am I the one…?

### 1.2:G26: Give-type verbs: where the recipient goes

A verb of giving (give, lend, send, owe, sell, pay, bring, teach, show) takes VERB THEME ala RECIPIENT. When the recipient is a pronoun it may instead stand bare straight after the verb: VERB PRONOUN THEME. Any other recipient takes ala.

- *Taivo guraga maivo dossa cono.* — You owe me two coins.
- *Fisa, rimara tressa cono ala maivo.* — Please lend me three coins.

### 1.2:G27: Which side of de is the owner

In X de Y the owner is Y, read as English "X of Y": li bumre de li cilni, the bread of the city; li lira de amor, the song of love. One exception: a pronoun or a personal name (pilo, father, used as a name included) standing before de is the owner, read as English "X's Y": maivo de lira, my song; pilo de bumre, father's bread. A pronoun is the owner wherever it stands: li purma de maivo, my parent. Fractions (tressa de cuara) are their own construction.

Refines 1.0:G7.

- *Taivo de lira ka vanisan.* — Your song is beautiful.
- *Li bitiri de pilo ke su li tabu.* — Father's tools are on the table.
- *Eli bumre ka sambasan ko pilo de bumre.* — This bread is the same as father's bread.
- *Li bumre de li cilni ka sambanasan da eli bumre.* — City bread is different from this bread.
- *Nullo de deyaapo, maivo po ne somusia.* — Last night, I didn't sleep.

### 1.2:G28: -ri after a number or quantifier

After a number or a quantifier the plural -ri is optional: the number already says how many. Choose by sound and meter. Without a number or quantifier, a plural noun takes -ri.

Refines 1.0:G7.

- *Li dossa veluri lumila.* — The two stars shine.
- *Taivo guraga maivo dossa cono.* — You owe me two coins.
- *Maivo po vimova muya zimziri en li folni.* — I saw many insects on the flower.
- *Li vila tovota muya marna.* — The village has much grain.

### 1.2:G29: An adjective standing as a noun

An adjective may stand as a noun phrase on its own only after an article or determiner (li, eli, ola, baba): li ramumomvisan, the forgotten. Without one, an adjective needs its noun.

Refines 1.0:G7.

- *yesta li raleri de li sadori de li ramumomvisan po lenila maivori de lira.* — even the echoes of the sorrows of the forgotten heard our song

### 1.2:G30: "About" is de

Liraasa has no word for "about / concerning". A song, story or word about something is a de phrase under G27: li lira de amor, the song of (about) love. After a verb of saying, give the verb an object rather than su: po sarbisia lirari de amor, wrote songs of love.

- *Li ralelirapatu de li lira ka melosan.* — The song's refrain is musical.
- *Li veluri po sarbisia lirari de amor.* — The stars wrote songs of love.

### 1.2:G31: Commands, address and narrative present

A command has no subject: the clause opens at the verb (slot 3 notes), softened with fisa. A person addressed is a noun phrase set off by commas, at the start or end of the clause. In verse, once the time has been set, the bare present may narrate past events.

- *Vulravelmava maivo de yendo, fisa.* — Bandage my palm, please.
- *Taivo viki pagopa ligo maivo ki, maivo de amor?* — Would you come near me, my love?

### 1.2:G32: Possession precedence

When de joins two candidates for owner, the owner is decided in this order: a pronoun, then a personal name (pilo, mare used as names included), then the default of 1.2:G27 (in X de Y, Y is the owner). Pilo de maivo is my father, not father's me. PRONOUN de PRONOUN is allowed, and the right-hand pronoun is the owner: taivo de maivo, you of mine.

Refines 1.2:G27.

- *Pilo de maivo po lirala.* — My father sang.
- *taivo de maivo* — you of mine

### 1.2:G33: yuta: the instrument

yuta + noun phrase names the instrument or means of an action: with, using, by means of. It fills the ADJUNCTS slot like the other prepositions. It never means accompaniment: "with a friend" stays ko.

- *Maivo lallela li dorbi yuta li kemye.* — I open the door with the key.
- *Li lirayo po yugemeyiya saivo yuta melo.* — The singer healed him with music.
- *Saivori po ka vazranasan yuta benamolu.* — They were defeated with kindness.
- *Maivo lirala ko saivo.* — I sing with him.

### 1.2:G34: Frequency correlatives

A quantifier joined to krono with a hyphen gives a frequency adverbial: muya-krono often, poka-krono rarely, rada-krono each time. Like the other -krono correlatives it opens or closes its clause (1.2:G24). The pattern is limited to krono; muya, poka and rada form no other correlatives.

Refines 1.2:G24.

- *Maivo lirala muya-krono.* — I sing often.
- *Rada-krono, li veluri lumila.* — Each time, the stars shine.

### 1.2:G35: Two more curated motion metaphors

**In part superseded by 2.5:G121:** its clause “the closed-list part” is replaced; the rest stands.

revo-sabesia (know again: recognize) and yave-donuda (give together: share) join revo-vimova and revo-lirala as curated metaphors. The motion prefixes otherwise stay non-productive.

- *Maivo revo-sabesia taivo.* — I recognize you.
- *Yave-donuda li bumre, fisa.* — Share the bread, please.

### 1.2:G36: Gender in kinship

Kinship words are neutral: purma parent, famli sibling, linu child, purmafamli aunt or uncle. Gender is marked only when it matters, by a compound with minva (female) or tarnu (male) where the lexicon has one (minvalinu daughter, tarnufamli brother), and otherwise by the adjective before the noun (minvasan purmafamli, aunt). mare and pilo remain mother and father. Kin by marriage compound with rilde: rildemare mother-in-law. Neither gender root is the negative of the other.

Refines ADR-0011.

- *Maivo de minvalinu lirala.* — My daughter sings.
- *Li tarnufamli de taivo ka corvisan.* — Your brother is brave.
- *minvasan purmafamli* — aunt
- *rildemare* — mother-in-law

## Rulings 1.0

### 1.0:G1: Scope by position

Every preverbal operator scopes over everything to its right. Pre-constituent operators (ne, doli, tu, yesta, pendi, vinto) scope over the single constituent that follows them.

### 1.0:G2: si and whether

si means only 'if'. 'whether' is ki-oro. 0.8 texts that used si for 'whether' are read under 0.8 rules.

### 1.0:G3: Permission vs possibility vs ability

povi = ability; nosi = epistemic possibility (might); dagi = permission (may, be allowed). 0.8's double use of nosi is split.

### 1.0:G4: Epistemic necessity

'must (logically)' = inferential evidential tosu + seye; musi is only obligation or circumstantial necessity.

### 1.0:G5: Intention

Intent is the verb chain 'dorvada sidu VERB' (intend to); kopi is only 'hope to'. Deliberately = la dorvasan; accidentally = la dorvanasan.

### 1.0:G6: Counterfactual

noma marks a clause as contrary to fact. 'should have' = po noma suvi; 'could have' = po noma povi; 'would have' = po viki (in a consequence clause) or po noma viki.

### 1.0:G7: Imminence

fututi in the approach slot means 'about to': Maivo po fututi pagopa = I was about to leave. As a clause-initial time word it still means 'soon'.

### 1.0:G8: Continuation

loki covers both 'still' and 'keep on'; no separate continuative is added.

### 1.0:G9: Quantity limits

Before a number or quantity, lusu = at least, mosa = at most, gase = approximately; pendi = exactly.

### 1.0:G10: Paired conjunctions

oro A oro B = either A or B; ne A ne B = neither A nor B; doli si = only if; yesta si = even if; 'si …, ata doli si …' = if and only if.

### 1.0:G11: Despite

nevi + noun phrase = despite: nevi li zeldo, despite the resistance. nevi + clause = although.

### 1.0:G12: Benefactive

vobi + noun phrase = for the benefit of / on behalf of (unchanged), and combines with any operator.

### 1.0:G13: ata

ata is 'and' only; additive 'also' is tu.

### 1.0:G14: Valency operators

vaku (make), lesvu (let) and tekvu (prevent) fill the verb slot of the main clause; the actor follows, then the actor's own [ne] [phase] [effort] verb. Tense, modal and evidential belong to the main clause only.

### 1.0:G15: Negation and operators

ne before an operator negates the operator; ne after it negates the event: po ne vazdi lirala = didn't manage to sing; po vazdi ne lirala = managed not to sing.

### 1.0:G16: Exclusions

At most one word per slot. hiro/teki do not combine with roni or tefa. tavki excludes fututi (same slot). The effort slot (telvo, vazdi, fesmo) excludes tefa. noma requires a tense, modal or si-clause context.

### 1.0:G17: Unexpectedly / previously / simultaneously

la vuzinasan = unexpectedly; kroti ola = previously; nayo ola = meanwhile/simultaneously; krotiapo = subsequently. No new words.

### 1.0:G18: Topic and focus together

A hozi-topic opens the clause; vinto may then mark a focus inside it. hozi and vinto never mark the same constituent.

### 1.0:G19: Version gating (reading older texts)

Texts dated before 1.0 are interpreted with the 0.8 lexicon and rules. A 1.0 reader meeting a retired spelling looks it up in legacy.retired and reads the 1.0 replacement.

### 1.0:G20: Lexicalised derivations

tisebitivu (sword), tibivu (rope), kaldopago (prison) and zeldoyo (rebel) are fixed meanings of regular derivations.

### 1.0:G21: Closing a relative clause

A raho-clause that does not end the sentence is closed by a comma in writing and a pause in speech: Li sabeyo raho vismava eli, ne yehu vimova li vordu. (Found in stress-testing: without the pause the main verb is read inside the relative clause.)

### 1.0:G22: Attitude words are clause-final only

seye, mufe, poye, pifu and zoke never open a clause. 'Perhaps, …' at the start of a sentence is written with the clause-final poye. (Found in stress-testing.)

### 1.0:G23: Events taking place

venreva (come) with an event as subject means 'take place, happen': Li dapadonu po venreva kroti li foji, the sale took place before winter.

## Rulings 0.8

### 0.8:R1: Source of truth and name (project)

The JSON is authoritative; the workbook is generated. Language field and files are named Liraasa.

### 0.8:R2: Predictable unused slots (project)

An unused form may receive a gloss only when its compositional meaning is predictable, approved case by case, and recorded in authorized_changes.

### 0.8:R3: Root Review queue (project)

Frozen for 0.8. No canonical root is respelled; the 40-root queue carries forward unchanged.

### 0.8:R4: Frame tense

A standalone tense particle opening a paragraph or turn (Po — / Futu —) sets a frame: following unmarked main clauses take that tense until another tense particle, pressa, or the end of the paragraph. Optional. Time words do not set a frame, so 0.7 texts keep their meaning.

### 0.8:R5: Article omission

li may be omitted in everyday speech as well as verse when the referent is obvious; never when ambiguous. baba may be omitted only in verse.

### 0.8:R6: sige

sige clauses never inherit tense; they are always marked. Tense inheritance applies to fime, raho, kube, nayo, kasa, kopisa and tuni.

### 0.8:R7: Yesterday evening (reading older texts)

deyaapo is kept as a fossil (it retains the retired past marker apo). apo is not used in new compounds; say vesno de deyaapo.

### 0.8:R8: Double -a verbs

amorliraa, amorrinbaa and amorvulraa are correct; each a is its own syllable.

### 0.8:R9: ki with certainty

A certainty marker (or zoke) precedes ki: Taivo lirala poye ki?

### 0.8:R10: Sacred register

Registers are defined in the grammar guide. The lexicon keeps everyday and poetic labels; sacred is added only to headwords whose root meaning is inherently sacred, after review.

### 0.8:R11: Headword target (project)

New vocabulary is sized by genuine gaps, not by the 120-150 target.

### 0.8:R12: -ti on names

The diminutive -ti may attach to personal names as an affectionate or teasing form (Dorinti).

### 0.8:G1: Demonstratives as pronouns

eli/ola (and eliri/olari) stand alone for things, situations and discourse (ola = what was just said; eli = what follows). For people use eli-nela/ola-nela or saivo, except presentational Eli ka NAME.

### 0.8:G2: Negation scope

ne precedes what it negates: verb or modal (after tense), copula (ne ka), noun phrase or fragment (Ne eli), or a contrasted constituent (ne X, nara Y). Negation is compositional: ne + nuna- is a positive (avoid).

### 0.8:G3: Ellipsis

Everyday speech may omit a repeated subject in coordinated clauses and in answers, omit a repeated predicate after a contrasting subject (Dorin, ne.), answer with tense + verb (Po sapusia.), and echo a word with ki. Speech may break off after any particle; the listener may complete it.

### 0.8:G4: Quotation and embedded questions

**In part superseded by 2.2:G60:** its clause “embedded wh-word stays in place” is replaced; the rest stands.

**In part superseded by 2.3:G77:** its clause “embedded yes/no questions use si (whether)” is replaced; the rest stands.

ki may close the main clause before an appended direct quotation (Taivo desida ki, fime maivo senosia: "...") as well as after it (0.7 order). Embedded wh-questions keep the wh-word in place with no ki; embedded yes/no questions use si (whether).

### 0.8:G5: Relative time in subordinate clauses

Inside an inheriting clause: tefa marks prior action, nayo/roni simultaneous, futu subsequent (future-in-past), sahi habitual.

### 0.8:G6: Nominalization

The root (form 1) is the noun of action or thing (juba, donu, tali). A verb phrase becomes a noun phrase with sidu (Sidu lisrola ka livna de taivo) or a clause with fime (Fime taivo po relnura ka baba juba).

### 0.8:G7: Noun-phrase order

[toda|rada|muya|poka|somi|nuna] [li|eli|ola|OWNER de] [NUMBER] [ADJ...] NOUN[-ti][-ri] [de ...] [raho ...]. toda N = every N; toda li N = all the N; toda OWNER de N = all OWNER's N.

### 0.8:G8: Sameness, difference, similarity

X ka sambasan ko Y (same as); X ka sambanasan da Y (different from); gase NP alone = like NP (simile); gase ADJ gase NP = as ADJ as NP. Lotero silda, ... = on the other hand.
