Part 1: Welcome

Liraasa is a constructed language built for singing: open syllables, pure vowels, penultimate stress and a regular morphology, so lyrics scan and rhyme predictably.

What this guide covers

This is the grammar of Liraasa 1.1: all of 1.0, the 1.1 vocabulary supplement, and the 1.2 grammar rulings. It starts with the sound, moves to words and the core vocabulary, then the small words and the clause, then recipes for turning English into Liraasa, every ruling, and finally how a machine reads a line.

It is written for three readers at once: the songwriter who writes and sings in Liraasa, a learner coming from English, and anyone rebuilding the parser, who needs the rules stated exactly.

How to read it

  • Liraasa is set in a serif face and its own colour, like lira, and is never re-spelled or hyphenated for display. Every example is a Liraasa line with its English underneath.
  • Each ruling carries a badge with its edition-qualified id, such as 1.0:G7 or 1.2:G27. G1–G8 exist in both the 0.8 and 1.0 ruling sets with different content, so a bare id is never used.
  • Two notes recur: a Singing note for meter, rhyme and free choices, and a Parsing note for how a machine decides.
  • Every example is canon, taken from the lexicon. Nothing here invents a word.

Syllables and stress

Every vowel is its own syllable, because adjacent vowels never merge. A consonant between vowels starts the next syllable; of two consonants, the first closes the syllable before. Stress falls on the second-to-last syllable. The capitals below mark it.

♪Singing note

The ascended forms in -asa and -asasan make a line sacred in register. Choose them for hymn-like moments; they add two syllables.

The verb rule

The verb is the only form that is not a plain suffix. It depends on the first letter of the word:

Root begins withVerbExample
opiopioaopi → opioa
n-initialR+nianedu → nedunia
s-initialR+siasabe → sabesia
vowel-initialR+aamor → amora
otherwiseR + first consonant + alira → lirala

opi, hope, is a real headword, so opioa is live data, not a reserved case. A compound takes the verb class of its first letter.

Suffixes and derivations

Order: ROOT + (one form OR one derivation) + -ti + -ri. A word takes one form or one derivation, then -ti, then -ri (settled question 4).

Compounds

A compound is MODIFIER + HEAD written together, head last: lunolumi is moon-light. Both components must be active headwords, and every compound has exactly two components, so its spelling is just the two joined. One compound is nested: mudusomubiti = mudu + somubiti.

A compound is one word, never two nouns side by side (ADR-0011). Its verb class follows its first letter.

Registers

  • Entries are everyday or poetic only. There is no sacred word list 0.8:R10.
  • Texts are everyday, poetic or sacred. Sacred is a property of the form (-asa, -asasan), not of the entry.
  • Irreverent is an optional tone flag on a text, never on a lexicon entry.

Retired spellings

63 roots were retired and 7 respelled in 1.0. Retired spellings are reserved until 2.0. A pre-1.0 text is read with the 0.8 lexicon 1.0:G19, and a parser warns on a pre-1.0 spelling and names its replacement.

Rulings 1.2 (G24–G31)

1.2:G24 Time words: opening or closing

A time word or time correlative (eli-krono, deyapressa, fututi, ki-krono's answers…) may open its clause or close it. When it opens the clause, the comma after it is optional. Only the four frame words zafra, finra, lanto and kenvi are opening-only, and they keep their comma. Refines 1.0 slot 1 FRAME.

Eli-krono, maivo lirala.
Now I sing.
Eli-krono maivo lirala.
Now I sing.
Saivo po venreva pendi eli-krono.
They arrived exactly now.
Zafra, li dorbi po lallela.
Suddenly the door opened.

1.2:G25 oro, alone and paired

A single oro joins two equal constituents, exactly as ata does: miru oro nami, water or food. It may also open a clause or line that continues the one before ("Or …"). The pair oro A oro B is the emphatic "either A or B". Refines 1.0:G10.

miru oro nami
water or food
oro maivo ke li suma ki?
Or am I the one…?

1.2:G26 Give-type verbs: where the recipient goes

A verb of giving (give, lend, send, owe, sell, pay, bring, teach, show) takes VERB THEME ala RECIPIENT. When the recipient is a pronoun it may instead stand bare straight after the verb: VERB PRONOUN THEME. Any other recipient takes ala.

Taivo guraga maivo dossa cono.
You owe me two coins.
Fisa, rimara tressa cono ala maivo.
Please lend me three coins.

1.2:G27 Which side of de is the owner

In X de Y the owner is Y, read as English "X of Y": li bumre de li cilni, the bread of the city; li lira de amor, the song of love. One exception: a pronoun or a personal name (pilo, father, used as a name included) standing before de is the owner, read as English "X's Y": maivo de lira, my song; pilo de bumre, father's bread. A pronoun is the owner wherever it stands: li purma de maivo, my parent. Fractions (tressa de cuara) are their own construction. Refines 1.0:G7.

Taivo de lira ka vanisan.
Your song is beautiful.
Li bitiri de pilo ka su li tabu.
Father's tools are on the table.
Eli bumre ka sambasan ko pilo de bumre.
This bread is the same as father's bread.
Li bumre de li cilni ka sambanasan da eli bumre.
City bread is different from this bread.
Nullo de deyaapo, maivo po ne somusia.
Last night, I didn't sleep.

1.2:G28 -ri after a number or quantifier

After a number or a quantifier the plural -ri is optional: the number already says how many. Choose by sound and meter. Without a number or quantifier, a plural noun takes -ri. Refines 1.0:G7.

Li dossa veluri lumila.
The two stars shine.
Taivo guraga maivo dossa cono.
You owe me two coins.
Maivo po vimova muya jemlari en li folni.
I saw many insects on the flower.
Li vila tovota muya marna.
The village has much grain.

1.2:G29 An adjective standing as a noun

An adjective may stand as a noun phrase on its own only after an article or determiner (li, eli, ola, baba): li ramumomvisan, the forgotten. Without one, an adjective needs its noun. Refines 1.0:G7.

yesta li raleri de li sadori de li ramumomvisan po lenila maivori de lira.
even the echoes of the sorrows of the forgotten heard our song

1.2:G30 "About" is de

Liraasa has no word for "about / concerning". A song, story or word about something is a de phrase under G27: li lira de amor, the song of (about) love. After a verb of saying, give the verb an object rather than su: po sarbisia lirari de amor, wrote songs of love.

Li ralelirapatu de li lira ka melosan.
The song's refrain is musical.
Li veluri po sarbisia lirari de amor.
The stars wrote songs of love.

1.2:G31 Commands, address and narrative present

A command has no subject: the clause opens at the verb (slot 3 notes), softened with fisa. A person addressed is a noun phrase set off by commas, at the start or end of the clause. In verse, once the time has been set, the bare present may narrate past events.

Vulravelmava maivo de yendo, fisa.
Bandage my palm, please.
Taivo viki pagopa ligo maivo ki, maivo de amor?
Would you come near me, my love?

Part 9: Parsing: how a machine reads a line

The reference implementation is the checker in the Workbench’s editor/index.html (parseWord, splitClauses, placeClause, checkLine, backTranslate). This part specifies it.

Data built from the lexicon

  • Form index: spelling → a list of readings. Some spellings belong to more than one namespace (maivo is a pronoun and a root).
  • Closed-class table: function words, correlatives, time words, particles, pronouns, colours and numerals.
  • Derivations: root + -yo / -vu / -pago; mavopago means only “society”.
  • Legacy table: pre-1.0 spelling → replacement. migration_forms.kind has five values; for archived phrases the target is a phrase, not a form.
  • English index: every glossed slot and every synonym.

Tokenising

Tokens are runs of letters, with internal hyphens and apostrophes kept: eli-krono, una-ne and dozira-cuara are one token each. Punctuation splits phrases. A comma is a pause, which matters for frames, for closing a relative clause 1.0:G21 and for address 1.2:G31.

Reading one word, in order

Lexicon first: many roots end in letters that look like suffixes (navu, sevu, pago), so a known spelling always beats a decomposition.

  1. Exact form in the form index → lexical (every reading kept).
  2. A closed-class word → its class.
  3. A numeral, plain or hyphenated, optionally with -ne (ordinal).
  4. A derivation (-yo, -vu, -pago).
  5. Suffix stripping (word longer than 4): strip -ri, then -ti if what remains is not known. The stem must be a known form, derivation or closed word.
  6. A motion prefix (va-, du-, taro-, yave-, revo-) on a verb form.
  7. A fraction, N-de-N.
  8. A legacy spelling → warn, naming the replacement.
  9. A name: capitalised, or in the known-name list.
  10. Otherwise: “not a word of Liraasa”, an error. Never guess a meaning.

Roles

  • From the word itself: tense, operator, copula, valency, determiner, quantifier, degree, focus, preposition, free, placed.
  • Lexical readings: verb slot → verb; root, ascended and negative → noun; the three adjective slots → adj.
  • Pronouns, names, derivations and fractions → noun. Place, time, manner and cause correlatives → adverb; the others → noun. Time words → adverb or noun.

Splitting clauses

A new clause starts at a linker, or at any ki- question word. A coordinated clause has its own single verb; an embedded one runs on into the main predicate, so its verb is not counted as a second verb.

ata nara oro si sina kasa kopisa nevi raho fime kube nayo tuni sige kureli sidu tonsi narli korsi krotiata yava gome ki-oro sabri

Placing tokens in slots

The parser is a state machine, start → subject → verb → post, walking left to right. Focus words stand before their constituent; free and placed words are skipped. Tense and operators move the state to verb. A preposition consumes the noun phrase after it, and la + adjective is an adjunct.

A time word opens or closes the clause freely 1.2:G24; after de it is a noun. The verb slot takes a verb form, a copula, a valency operator or gome. A noun phrase before the verb is the subject; after it, the object or complement. Give-type verbs take two bare noun phrases only when the first is a pronoun 1.2:G26. Anything left over is an error naming the token and the slot expected.

Reading de

In X de Y, Y is the owner, “X of Y”, unless X is a pronoun or a personal name, in which case X is the owner, “X’s Y” 1.2:G27. A pronoun is the owner on either side. NUMBER de NUMBER is a fraction. de needs a noun phrase on both sides.

E. Discourse particles

hema

hesitation; holding the floor (&quot;well…&quot;, &quot;um&quot;). Where: clause-initial; between constituents; standalone. Register: everyday (also poetic for hesitant speech; not sacred).

Hema… maivo ne sabesia.
Well… I don't know.
Taivo futu relnura ki? — Hema. Poye.
Will you come back? — Well. Maybe.
Maivo vurlova, hema, baba cupa de miru.
I'd like, um, a cup of water.
Hema, hema… li bumre ne keleka.
Well, well… the bread isn't rising.

nido

self-repair: cancels the speaker's own preceding word, phrase or clause and replaces it (&quot;I mean&quot;, &quot;rather&quot;, &quot;no —&quot;). Where: immediately after the abandoned item and before its replacement; or clause-initial to reformulate a whole previous utterance. Register: everyday; poetic (escalating correctio).

Maivo po bumreba tressa, nido, cuara bumreri.
I baked three, I mean, four loaves.
Taivo ka benasan. Nido, taivo ka ruli benasan.
You're good. I mean, you're very good.
Maivo amora taivo, nido, maivo devoda taivo.
I love you, no, I pledge myself to you.
Maivo po bumreba ne tressa, nara cuara bumreri.
I baked not three but four loaves. (corrective negation, for contrast)

yori

topic return / closure (&quot;anyway&quot;, &quot;at any rate&quot;); with a possessive seno phrase, resumption (&quot;as I was saying&quot;). Where: clause-initial; standalone. Register: everyday.

Yori. Li bumre keleka ki?
Anyway. Is the bread rising?
Yori, maivo de seno: li bumrepago nedunia cono.
As I was saying: the bakery needs coins.
Yori, taivo de seno. Taivo po senosia fime pilo po lenila maivo.
Back to what you were saying. You said father heard me.
Yori.
Whatever. / Anyway.

zoke

irony / sarcasm: the speaker does not endorse, or mocks, the proposition. Where: clause-final, in the attitude slot (instead of seye/mufe/poye/pifu); may precede ki; standalone. Register: everyday only.

Dorin milobema toda-krono, zoke.
Dorin smiles all the time. (Sure he does.)
Ola ka li bumreasasan, zoke.
That is divinely bready. (Oh, sure.)
Zoke.
Oh, sure. (eye-roll)
Taivo po bumreba eli, zoke ki?
YOU baked this, did you?
