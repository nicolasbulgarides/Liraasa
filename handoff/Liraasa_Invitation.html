These two lines come from Nemelira de devi, The Goddess’s Lament. A goddess sings to the one she loves, who fell from the sky and became mortal. By the end of this booklet you will read the whole song, word by word.

The name

liraasa “song of the first stars”

The word for song is lira. Raised to its highest degree it becomes liraasa, song of the first stars, and that is the language’s name. Four words carry most of its imagery: sola sun, lore heart, luno moon, lira song.

Liraasa is a language centred on a goddess, and it was built for singing. Its vowels are pure, most of its words end on an open vowel, and every word leans on the same syllable, so a melody always knows where to land.

Words English doesn’t have

Some feelings take English a whole sentence. Liraasa gives them a word, and each word is two older words joined, so you can see how the meaning was made.

  • loterotelne lotero other + telne story the realization that every stranger lives a story as deep as your own
  • lorevuzi lore heart + vuzi expectation the sense that one is about to fall in love
  • bakovani bako break + vani beauty the beauty of broken and imperfect things
  • aurumimeyi aurumi yellow + meyi mend mending with gold; a wound made beautiful
  • lasomambi laso peaceful quiet + mambi moment a meaningful pause; the space between notes
  • nemeamor neme sorrow + amor love grief remade as love
  • vanineme vani beauty + neme sorrow the sorrow of beautiful things passing
  • momvilavi momvi loss + lavi joy joy after loss
  • sabesuru sabe knowing + suru feeling feeling understood
  • melolavi melo music + lavi joy ecstasy brought on by music

How it sounds

Liraasa is spelled exactly as it is sung. Nothing is silent, and nothing changes its sound from one word to the next.

  • Five pure vowels, a e i o u, as in Italian or Spanish. Every vowel is its own syllable: daigo is da-i-go, three syllables.
  • Three letters to watch. c is always “ch”: cilni, city, is “chil-ni”. r is a single tap of the tongue, as in Spanish pero. g is always hard, as in “go”: gase.
  • The weight falls on the second-to-last syllable: LI-ra, lu-no-LU-mi, ma-I-vo.

Every word falls from its strong syllable to a light last one, and the small words are light all through. Put the strong syllables on the strong beats and the line sings itself.

A singer’s freedom

In song, neighbouring vowels may share one note. daigo can be sung da-i-go or dai-go; navuasa, the firmament, na-vu-a-sa or na-vua-sa. The spelling never changes. The melody chooses.

One root, seven words

Every root in Liraasa can take seven shapes. Here is daigo, death, in all of them:

  • the thing itselfdaigodeath
  • raised to its highest -asadaigoasaultimate death, final death
  • turned around -nadaigonaresurrection
  • to do itdaigodato die
  • describing -sandaigosanmortal
  • describing, raised -asasandaigoasasanof the final death
  • describing, turned -nasandaigonasandeath-ending, healing, life-giving; deathless; immortal

The shapes are made by rule, but each meaning is the language’s own: the highest degree of death is the final death, and death turned around is daigona, resurrection.

Not every root fills every shape. A shape is a word only when the language has given it a meaning: garvu, lion, has no verb, and garvuga stays empty.

Words from words

Two words make a third. The second word is what the thing is; the first tells you what kind.

luno moon + lumi light = lunolumi moonlight

The Lament is full of them:

  • sola sun + nevra descent = solanevra sunset
  • krono time + rivla flow = kronorivla the flow of time
  • velu star + melo music = velumelo the music of the spheres
  • ramu memory + duha longing = ramuduha longing for what is gone
  • velu star + solo stone = velusolo meteorite; a fallen star-stone

A word made this way can be used again. mavolumi, soul, is mavo mind + lumi light: the light of the mind. Add miga, relation, and you have mavolumimiga, a soulbond.

The image engine

Some words join so often that Liraasa lets them pair freely. Take any of 57 words for a colour, a season or a feeling, and any of 31 words for a thing: every pair is a word, 1,767 in all, and you can read its meaning straight off its parts.

The first word lends its colour; the second is the thing. So lunosavali, moon + kiss, is the moonlit kiss of the Lament, and here are a few more:

  • veluvulra starlit + woundstarlit wound; a wound that remembers distant stars
  • fojosavali snow-softened + kisssnow-softened kiss; an intimate kiss recalling falling snow
  • veluvilne starlit + eyestarlit eyes; eyes brightened or clouded by distant stars
  • sevuhavli sea-borne + breathsea-borne breath; a breath filled with the sea
  • norunulu dream-born + pathdream-born path; a trail shaped by dreams
  • ramubelda remembered + bellremembered bell; a bell stirred by memory
  • vesnolira twilight-soft + songtwilight-soft song; a melody given the color of evening
  • lumimanli luminous + handluminous hand; hands marked by light

Try it

Join each pair into one word, and say what it means.

  1. merno dawn-fresh + lira song = ?
  2. noru dream-born + havli breath = ?
  3. fojo snow-softened + belda bell = ?

Answers: 1. mernolira: dawn-fresh song; a melody given the color of morning · 2. noruhavli: dream-born breath; a breath filled with dreams · 3. fojobelda: snow-softened bell; a bell stirred by falling snow

Just enough grammar to read a song

The order is English order

Who, then what happens, then to what. The describing word comes first: daigosan yuge, a mortal body.

Two ways to say “is”

ka is for what is so by nature; ke is for what is so for now. English needs a phrase for what Liraasa says in one small word.

“Not” goes first

ne stands just before what it denies. Before povi, can, it makes cannot.

A question ends in ki

Say it as a statement, then add ki.

Past, future, and “of”

Past and future are small words

A verb never changes its ending for time. po before it means it happened; futu means it will. With neither, it is happening now.

Whose is it?

de means of. Usually the owner comes second, as in English “the bread of the city”. But when the first word is a person, I, you or someone’s name, that person is the owner.

The centrepiece

Nemelira de devi, word by word

The Goddess's Lament again, now with its meaning under every word. ASC marks a word raised to its highest, PL more than one.

  1. 1

    MaivoI deof amor,love taivoyou po(past) nevraniadescend dafrom lithe navuasasky-ASC alato lithe teruearth gaseas babaa velusolo.meteorite

    My love, you descended from the firmament to the earth like a falling star-stone.

    Maivo de amor, my love: the Goddess is speaking. navuasa is navu, sky, raised to its highest: the firmament. gase baba …, like a …, makes a simile.

  2. 2

    Taivoyou po(past) yugeyaembody babaa daigosanmortal yuge,body ataand lithe veluristar-PL po(past) lasola.hush

    You took on a mortal body, and the stars fell silent.

    A mortal body: the describing word comes first.

  3. 3

    Eli-krononow taivoyou keis (now) lemvosantired enin lithe cilniricity-PL deof lithe nelari.person-PL

    Now you are weary in the cities of humankind.

    -ri makes more than one: cilni, city, cilniri, cities; nela, person, nelari, people.

  4. 4

    Taivoyou deof mavolumisoul kais (always) velusan,starlit narabut taivoyou deof yugebody keis (now) vulrasan.wounded

    Your soul is starlit by nature, but your body is wounded for now.

    ka by nature, ke for now.

  5. 5

    MaivoI vudain a dream vimovasee taivoyou deof timruritear-PL rahowhich ralnorafall gaseas renli.rain

    In dreams I see your tears, which fall like rain.

    vuda tells you how the singer knows: she sees this in a dream. raho, which, begins a description of the tears.

  6. 6

    Lithe harmavurlotemptationnow harmadesi tivrotahold taivo,you ataand lithe kronorivlaflow of time nenot teklata.stop

    Temptation holds you, and the flow of time does not stop.

    ne comes right before what it denies.

  7. 7

    Taivoyou lemulalearn lithe lorenuravuanguish deof lithe nelariperson-PL ataand lithe vemineme.world-weariness

    You learn the anguish of humankind and the sorrow of the world.

    lorenuravu, heart + ache, is anguish; vemineme, world + sorrow, is world-weariness.

  8. 8

    Taivoyou deof manlirihand-PL yanbaya,age ataand taivoyou deof loreheart keis (now) lemvosan.tired

    Your hands grow old, and your heart is weary.

  9. 9

    MaivoI lavbulapray vobifor taivoyou hupiabove lithe sulbanevra,sunsetnow solanevra narabut maivoI deof vallavoice keis (now) lasosan.silent

    I pray for you above the sunset, but my voice is silent.

    vobi, for, and hupi, above, come before what they govern. solanevra, sun + going down, is sunset; the song, written before the word was respelled, spells it sulbanevra.

  10. 10

    Kasabecause maivoI kais (always) devi,goddess maivoI nenot povican venrevacome alato taivo.you

    Because I am a goddess, I cannot come to you.

    ne povi: cannot.

    Death is a door, and I wait for you in the Divine Light.

    My longing for what is gone is as deep as the dark between the stars.

    gase … gase … is “as … as …”. vamrisevu, space + sea, is the dark between the stars.
