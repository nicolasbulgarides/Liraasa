# Review of the Everyday and Fiction gap report, and a proposed Liraasa 3.1 patch

*Proposed. Built on Liraasa 3.0 (2.5 plus the 3.0 patch: 1,590 headwords). Nothing is canon until you ratify it.*

## Verdict

The report is a good piece of work. Its method is sound, its caution is right, and its main conclusion is correct: the next gains are mundane actions and sensory distinctions, not more transcendent vocabulary. I agree with almost all of its A-priority list. The places I disagree are mostly where it under-counted what the language already has.

**I checked all 129 candidates** against the 3.0 lexicon, including two tables the report appears not to have searched (`lexicalised_derivations` and `lexicalised_diminutives`) and the curated motion-prefix meanings:

| Disposition | Senses | Of the A list | Of the B list | Of the C list |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3.1 tier 1 | 36 | 34 | 2 | 0 |
| 3.1 tier 2 | 52 | 0 | 50 | 2 |
| Already in the lexicon | 27 | 1 | 22 | 4 |
| Already sayable (recipe) | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| Deferred | 13 | 0 | 7 | 6 |

### Where I agree

- **The A list is real.** 34 of its 36 senses have no word today. Only *recognize* is already covered, and *realize* needs a recipe rather than a word.
- **Body, face, touch and kitchen are the weakest areas.** Chewing, swallowing, squinting, slipping, squeezing, a fork, a shelf, a towel and a spill all had no word. Those are exactly the things fiction narrates on every page.
- **The protocol is right:** document a construction before coining a synonym, prefer compounds to roots, and keep the 7-form system unfilled.

### Where I disagree

1. **Many "gaps" are already words.** The B and C place list is almost entirely covered by listed derivations:
   - places: *kaldopago* (prison), *juseyo* (judge), *lemupago* (school), *bullipago* (library), *helnapago* (hospital), *gonbipago* (inn), *buzapago* (tavern), *botepago* (harbor), *taradapago* (market);
   - landscape diminutives and compounds: *monruti* (hill), *kezumiruti* (pond), *mirugalsa* (marsh);
   - other senses: *revo-sabesia* (recognize), *amorharlu* (romantic jealousy, so the relational/envy contrast already exists against *loterodesi*), *silrosenosia* (gossip), *zivu* (contemptuous sneer), *lelsu* (shudder), *vopra* (twist), *hunrasan*/*hunranasan* (ripe/unripe), *ceresan* (clear), *nilminasan* (rough), *lisisan* (greasy), *filpana* (unfolding), *vilnesevre* (blink).
2. **"A dozen well-chosen words" is too small a target.** The A list alone needs about 34 words, because body actions do not substitute for each other: chewing is not swallowing. They are cheap, though. Of 88 additions, 83 are transparent compounds, derivations or diminutives of existing words. Only five roots are new.
3. **The report missed one basic word.** Testing its own suspense sentence showed that Liraasa has no word for **plan**. It is added as *dorvalordi* ("an ordered intention").

## The 3.1 patch

The patch is **lexical only: no new grammar.**

- **Tier 1 (35 entries):** the genuine A-list gaps. Ratify these first.
- **Tier 2 (53 entries):** B-list senses that a short compound fills cleanly. Each can be ratified, deferred or dropped individually.

After the patch: **1664 headwords, 4404 meaningful forms**.

### Five new roots

Each is a foundational sense that no compound states cleanly, and each one generalizes across many words:

| Root | Forms | Why a root |
|---|---|---|
| **dugo** | *dugo* decay; rot; *dugoda* to rot; to spoil; to decay; *dugosan* rotten; spoiled; stale (of food); *dugonasan* unspoiled; incorruptible | decay underlies rotten, stale, rust (germadugo), mold (herbadugo); its negative dugonasan, incorruptible, also serves devotion |
| **pega** | *pega* stickiness; adhesion; *pegapa* to stick; to adhere; *pegasan* sticky | adhesion is basic physics and texture (sticky, to stick); no existing root implies clinging |
| **toze** | *toze* poison; venom; *tozeta* to poison; *tozesan* poisonous; venomous; *tozena* antidote; *tozenasan* harmless; non-toxic | poison is a pivot of mystery, fantasy and safety; its negative noun tozena (antidote) follows the daigona pattern |
| **bika** | *bika* flinch; a start; an involuntary recoil; *bikaba* to flinch; to startle; *bikasan* jumpy; easily startled | the startle reflex is distinct from trembling (zego), shivering (lelsu) and fear (follu) |
| **tuzo** | *tuzo* hanging; suspension; *tuzota* to hang; to suspend; *tuzosan* hanging; suspended | hanging and suspension (lamps, bells, gallows, fruit); tier 2 |

### Already sayable: recipes added instead of words

| English | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| recognize (someone or something met before) | revo-sabesia (listed prefix meaning, 2.5:G121) | *Maivo revo-sabesia taivo.* I recognize you. |

## The report's eight stress-test sentences, in 3.1

All eight now translate without circumlocution. Each passes the lexicon and word-order validator.

| Liraasa | English | Tests |
|---|---|---|
| Sidu patudonupa li patu raho lisrola ka maivo de lorelivna, ne sidu livnala baba lorelivnasan. | To share the piece that remains is my preference, not to choose a favorite. | prefer, share, favorite |
| Saivo po lurvaralnola li lavlomiru, po vizutivrova baba safavelmavu, ata po sispasia li pegasan tobratabu. | She spilled the tea, grabbed a towel, and wiped the sticky shelf. | spill, grab, towel, sticky, shelf |
| Saivo po dentosapuda la vizunasan, po sapunevrasia, ata po nuravufasbinia. | He chewed slowly, swallowed, and winced. | chew, swallow, wince |
| Saivo po vilnelendava ala li lumimellinasan zupanulu, ata po helvivimoha nazru li domluvelso. | She squinted into the dim corridor and peeked behind the curtain. | squint, dim, peek, curtain |
| Saivo po ne loterodesila li loteronela; saivo po amorharlua saivo de devorivo. | She did not envy the stranger; she was jealous over her partner. | envy (2.5) vs jealousy (restored verb) |
| Li buzakova raho po gome tozeta po ke baba helvihata, nara saivo po silrosudesia li dorvalordi. | The cup that had been poisoned was an ambush, but she uncovered the plan. | poison, ambush, reveal, plan |
| Maivo po loteromebanela taivo, nara eli-krono, maivo hiro sabesia raho taivo po desida sidu senosia. | I misunderstood you, but now I realize what you meant. | misunderstand; realize = hiro sabesia |
| Saivo po lisiralnola su li mulyisan teru, po pebusemnepa, ata po dozativroda saivo de pebu. | He slipped on the wet floor, crouched, and squeezed his foot. | slip, crouch, squeeze |

Two small concessions:
- Sentence 6 says "was an ambush" rather than "was meant as an ambush".
- Sentence 8 says "foot", because there is still no word for *ankle*. Ankle, wrist, elbow and other joint words are a natural next audit.

## Decisions for you

1. **The five roots.**
   - *dugo* (decay), *pega* (sticky), *toze* (poison) and *bika* (flinch) are the ones I would defend most strongly.
   - *tuzo* (hang) is tier 2.
   - All five are at least two edits from every 3.0 word and use the "bodily/everyday" consonants of 2.1:G57, not the lyrical ones kept for the sacred.
2. **A few compounds whose meaning is a judgment call:**
