# EN: About this edition
# EN: Beneath the play now sits one question: what can be sold, what can only be given, and
# EN: what can only be let go? The bakery changes hands by sale, the bread by gift, the song by
# EN: being heard, and the father, it turns out, by letting his daughter leave. Liraasa 1.0 has a
# EN: separate word or operator for each of those acts, so the play can finally argue about them
# EN: in its own language.
# EN: The story, the three voices and the shape of the night are unchanged. 165 of the 231 turns
# EN: are word for word the original play; 66 are new or rewritten, including the 18 lines the plain
# EN: 1.0 edition already changed. Each turn below gives the Liraasa with the English beneath it;
# EN: marks a line that differs from the original.
# EN: Four ideas carry the subtext, and each is built into the grammar:
# EN: 1. Owning is three things. lanvetovo (owned by law), tovo (held) and nimu (belonging,
# EN: home). Kipo becomes the legal owner; the bakery stays Aviya's home. Her question
# EN: Maivo dagi lisrola ki? ("Am I allowed to stay?") turns on the 1.0 permission modal, and
# EN: Kipo's answer separates the law from the home.
# EN: 2. Giving is three things, then four. juba (gift), donu (handing-over) and dapadonu (sale).
# EN: Kipo, who counts everything, names three; Aviya adds a fourth he never counted,
# EN: tivromomvi (letting go). That is what the father gave.
# EN: 3. Love as permission, not cause. 1.0's vaku / lesvu / tekvu (make / let / prevent) let Dorin
# EN: say the play's revelation exactly: father did not make Aviya leave; he let her, and he
# EN: prevented the telling.
# EN: 4. The invisible cause. In Movement I, Kipo jokes that no one sees the yeast work: Nuna-
# EN: nela vimova li vordu; toda-nela sapusia li yordo ("No one sees the cause; everyone eats
# EN: the effect"). At the climax Aviya answers it: Pilo ka li kelesamni ("Father is the yeast").
# EN: The play also runs on three ways of knowing, marked by evidentials: Dorin knew by
# EN: sight (yehu), Kipo by report (buyu), and Aviya, at last, by inference (tosu).
# EN: And one refusal of regret: Aviya says Maivo po noma suvi relnura ("I should have come
# EN: back"), and Kipo answers that what would have happened is bumrena, no-bread, his
# EN: Movement I joke turned into the play's quietest argument: counterfactuals do not rise.
